## 1. Why Japan, and why now, for Indian travellers

Some countries you visit. Japan you *experience* — and it rewards Indians more generously than almost any long-haul destination on earth.

Here's the case in one breath: the flight is short (7.5–8.5 hours nonstop from Delhi/Mumbai), the jetlag is negligible (Japan is only **3.5 hours ahead of IST**, so you lose one groggy morning, not three days), the visa is astonishingly cheap for us, the country is spotlessly clean and the safest you'll ever feel, the trains run to the *second*, and — thanks to a historically weak yen — world-class food, whisky and shopping are a relative bargain. In mid-2026, **100 yen ≈ ₹59** (1 JPY ≈ ₹0.59), so the mental math is easy: a yen figure is **roughly 60% of itself in rupees** (¥10,000 ≈ ₹5,900). Re-check the rate before you fly.

And it's not intimidating. Shrines welcome all faiths, English signage covers the tourist trail, Google Translate's camera reads menus in real time, and ordering is easy once you learn the ticket machines and plastic food displays (Section 6). Best of all, in **October** you land into the finest weather of the year — mild, dry days made for walking.

Indian arrivals to Japan hit a record in 2025 (reported around 315,000, up sharply year-on-year — figures vary by source, so treat as indicative). You're not a pioneer anymore; you're joining a wave.

> **The one-line reassurance for nervous relatives:** Japan is 3.5 hours ahead, the crime rate is near-zero, tap water is drinkable, and you'll be home in under 9 hours of flying. Go.

## 2. How many days? 7, 10 and 14-day itineraries

**The honest answer:** the practical minimum worth the airfare is **7 days on the ground**; the sweet spot is **10–14 days**. Because jetlag is mild, a "7-day trip" genuinely gives you ~7 usable days — you can start sightseeing the afternoon you land.

| Trip length | What it realistically covers | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 3–4 days | One city only (e.g. just Tokyo) | Too short to justify the flight |
| **7 days** | Tokyo + Kyoto + Osaka (compact Golden Route) | One region done well — the minimum |
| **10 days** | Golden Route + Hakone/Fuji + a Hiroshima day | The most-recommended length |
| **14 days** | The "Grand Tour" — adds Hiroshima/Miyajima overnight, *or* a Central-Japan (Kanazawa–Takayama–Shirakawa-go) loop | Ideal comprehensive first trip |
| 14+ days | Add Hokkaido or Okinawa as a *separate fly-in leg* | Only if you have the leave |

**Rule of thumb for building your own:** ~3 nights Tokyo, ~3 nights Kyoto (with a Nara day trip), 1–2 nights Osaka, then add one "themed block" (Hakone, Hiroshima, or Kanazawa) per extra 2–3 days you have. Japan rewards depth over a checklist sprint.

**Pro move — fly "open-jaw":** into Tokyo (Narita/Haneda), out of Osaka (Kansai/KIX). It saves a 2.5-hour backtrack and often costs about the same as a return. **One catch:** KIX has no nonstop from India (Section 4), so your home leg from Osaka will be a one-stop connection — price the open-jaw against a Tokyo-return-with-nonstop, not against a fantasy KIX nonstop.

> **🍁 Going in October? Add a foliage leg.** The Golden-Route cities (Tokyo/Kyoto/Osaka) will still be **green** in October, so if chasing autumn colour, bolt on one of: **Nikko** (peaks late Oct, easiest — a day/overnight from Tokyo), the **Japanese Alps** (Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route / Kamikochi, mid–late Oct), or a **Hokkaido** fly-in (first half of Oct). See the timing map in Section 7.

> **Multigenerational tip:** many first Indian trips are with parents and kids. This route is very senior/kid-friendly — lifts at major stations, luggage-forwarding (Section 13) so nobody hauls bags up stairs, and short hops. At shrines with gravel/steps, a **baby carrier beats a stroller**; strollers are fine in cities and malls.

This loop passes the Japanese Alps, so it doubles as your **October foliage leg**. Use the **JR Takayama-Hokuriku Area Pass**, not the nationwide JR Pass.

## 3. Best time to visit: seasons and why October works

You've picked one of the two best windows in the Japanese year. Japan has two "golden" seasons — cherry blossoms (spring) and autumn foliage (autumn) — both gorgeous, but the priciest and most crowded. **October threads the needle:** the summer heat and humidity are gone, the skies are clear, the big November foliage crush hasn't started, and — for Indians — it lines up neatly with the Diwali/Dussehra break. Your full October game plan (foliage timing, festivals, packing) is in **Section 7**; here's the year in context.

| Season | Weather | Highlights | Crowds & cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spring** (late Mar–Apr) | ~20°C day, ~5°C night, changeable | Cherry blossoms, festivals | PEAK — book months ahead |
| **Summer** (Jun–Aug) | 31°C *feeling like 40°C+*, humid; June rain, Sep typhoons | Fireworks (hanabi), Gion Matsuri, Nebuta | Hot & pricey; hardest to travel |
| **Autumn** (Oct–Nov) | 14–25°C, clear, dry | Fiery foliage, temple light-ups, autumn foods | **Best balance** — comfortable, moderate |
| **Winter** (Dec–Feb) | ~10°C Tokyo, sub-zero north | Snow, onsen, ski, Sapporo Snow Festival | **Cheapest** outside New Year |

**The one October caveat (important):** autumn colour (*koyo*) peaks in October **only in the north and up high** — Hokkaido, the Japanese Alps, Nikko. The famous red maples of **Kyoto and Tokyo don't peak until mid-to-late November**, so they'll still be green in October. Plan around this (Section 7 has the full timing map) rather than expecting Kyoto to be scarlet.

### ⛔ Periods to AVOID (crowds + prices spike, seats sell out)
| Period | When (confirm each year) |
|---|---|
| **Golden Week** (busiest of all) | ~29 Apr – early May |
| **Obon** (travel crush) | ~13–16 Aug |
| **New Year** (widespread closures) | ~29 Dec – 3 Jan |
| **Sports Day long weekend** (October crowd spike — see §7) | Fri–Mon around **Oct 10–12, 2026** |

> **India-specific:** the **Diwali/autumn break** is the single best fit for an Indian holiday — mild, dry, spectacular early foliage up north, no domestic shutdown, and a blessed escape from anyone who finds Japanese summer oppressive. Just watch the fare timing (Section 4) and Japan's own Sports Day weekend (Section 7).

## 4. Getting there: flights and visa from India

### Flights (fares in ₹)

Direct nonstop options from India: **Delhi–Tokyo** (Air India, ANA, JAL, ~7.5h) and **Mumbai–Tokyo/Haneda** (ANA; Air India also operates a Mumbai–Haneda nonstop launched in 2026, ~8h15m). **There is currently no scheduled nonstop from Bengaluru or from Osaka/KIX** — for those you connect via Dubai/Singapore/Bangkok/Hong Kong. (Route maps change; verify nonstop availability on the airline site for your dates.)

| Ticket type | Typical round-trip fare |
|---|---|
| One-stop economy (VietJet, Vietnam Airlines, Cathay, Singapore) | **₹52,000–65,000** (cheapest deals ₹43,000–52,000) |
| Nonstop Air India (DEL–NRT / BOM–HND) | **₹68,000–90,000** |
| Premium full-service (JAL/ANA) in peak season | ₹88,000–1,05,000+ |

A safe "typical" figure for budgeting: **₹55,000–65,000 round-trip** on a one-stop economy ticket; a well-timed one-stop deal can dip to ~₹45,000. Use an overnight departure from India so you land Tokyo around midday and gain a bonus half-day.

> **⏱️ October fare timing (read this — it's the money move):** October is *not* the priciest month on the **Japan side** (spring and peak-November are dearer). The catch is on the **India side**: October–November is India's festival travel crunch — **Dussehra falls ~Oct 20, 2026 and Diwali ~Nov 8, 2026** — and outbound fares can jump **50–150% in the final two weeks** before, climbing from ~30 days out. So: **book ~60–90 days ahead (by roughly mid-August)**, earlier still if you fly across the Dussehra/Diwali window. **Early October is the sweet spot** — you get genuine foliage up north *and* dodge the Indian-festival fare surge. Given October's typhoon tail (Section 7), prefer **refundable/flexible** tickets.

### Visa for Indians

Japan has historically been one of the cheapest long-haul visas an Indian passport can get — a nominal consular fee (long quoted at a flat **₹500**), well below what many nationalities pay.

**Important verify note:** Japan revised visa fees worldwide with effect from **1 July 2026**. Before you apply, **confirm the current single- and multiple-entry fees for Indian nationals on the Embassy of Japan in India / VFS Global fee schedule** — do not assume last year's figure or a blanket exemption. The figures below are indicative and must be re-checked.

| Item | Cost / detail (verify current) |
|---|---|
| Consular (embassy) fee — Indians | historically **₹500** (single or multiple entry) — reconfirm |
| VFS service charge | ~₹800 (incl. taxes) |
| **All-in via VFS** | **~₹1,300** (+ optional courier ~₹350–550) |
| eVisa route | ~₹500 online, no VFS charge (single-entry tourism) |
| Processing | ~5–7 working days (apply 3–4 weeks ahead; 8–10 in peak) |
| Validity | Single-entry ~3 months from issue, stay up to 90 days; multiple-entry 1–5 years |

**The eVisa** (available for India since 2024) is applied for online via VFS Global / an accredited agency; on approval you get an electronic **"Visa Issuance Notice"** — no passport sticker. **Critical:** at the airport you must show it *live on your phone with internet* — printouts, PDFs and screenshots are NOT accepted. So keep an eSIM/roaming ready the moment you land.

**Document checklist:** passport (6+ months validity, 2 blank pages), form, two 45×45mm white-background photos, cover letter, confirmed flight + hotel bookings, 6-month bank statements + ITR/salary proof, employer NOC/leave letter. The #1 rejection trigger is a last-minute large deposit — keep your balance consistent (~₹3–5 lakh for a 10–15 day trip). Use a *flight reservation* (hold), not a paid ticket, until approved.

> **The postcard Fuji shot — how to actually get it (October is good!):**
> - **Kawaguchiko** (Fuji Five Lakes): direct **highway bus from Shinjuku** (~1h45–2h, ~¥2,200) or the **Fuji Excursion** limited express. Lakeside Fuji views, ropeway, and boat.
> - **Chureito Pagoda** (the pagoda-with-Fuji frame): take the Fujikyu line to **Shimo-Yoshida** station, then ~10 min walk plus ~400 steps up.
> - **October bonus:** Fuji usually gets its **first snow cap in early October**, and clear, dry autumn mornings give better odds than the cloud-hidden summer. Still keep a plan B — go early, just after sunrise.

### ⛩️ Kyoto & Nara

The universal rule: **arrive at opening (6:00–8:30 AM)** — every marquee site is calm before 8 and mobbed 9 AM–4 PM. Temples are cheap (~¥500/₹295) but **carry cash** — cards are unreliable at temple windows. (In October the gardens are lush green, not red — but far less crowded than the November peak.)

| Kyoto site | Fee | Timing tip |
|---|---|---|
| **Fushimi Inari** (10,000 torii gates) | Free, 24/7 | Before 7 AM for empty photos; full loop is 2–3h |
| **Kinkaku-ji** (Golden Pavilion) | ¥500 (~₹295) | Right at 9 AM for the gold reflection |
| **Kiyomizu-dera** (hillside stage) | ¥500 | Opens 6 AM — a rare early-beat-the-crowds site |
| **Arashiyama Bamboo Grove** | Free | Before 8 AM (no lighting, no toilets inside) |
| **Ginkaku-ji** (Silver Pavilion) | ¥1,000 (~₹590; raised in 2026 — verify) | Pair with the free Philosopher's Path |
| **Gion** (geisha district) | Free streets | ⚠️ **¥10,000 fine** for entering private alleys/photographing geiko |

**Nara day trip** (~45 min, ~¥720/₹425): free Nara Park with ~1,000 bowing deer (crackers ¥200), **Todai-ji's Great Buddha**, Kasuga Taisha's lanterns. Bow to a deer and it bows back — then show empty palms so it moves on. Keep tickets/maps hidden (deer eat paper).

*Book tea ceremonies (Camellia, ¥3,300 group) and kimono rental (~¥5,000–6,000/day) weeks ahead in autumn.*

### 🏯 Osaka & the West (Kansai + Chugoku)

Base yourself in **Namba** (walkable to Dotonbori & Kuromon, direct Nankai line to KIX).

- **Osaka Castle** — ¥1,200 (~₹710); free park & photogenic exterior. Go at 09:00.
- **Dotonbori & Kuromon Market** — the "eat till you drop" heart (Section 6).
- **Universal Studios Japan + Super Nintendo World** — a full day. Studio Pass from ¥8,900 (~₹5,075); grab the *free* timed-entry slot in the USJ app the moment you tap in, or pay for an Express Pass.
- **Kobe** — genuine A5 Kobe beef teppanyaki; do it as a **lunch** (Steakland from ¥3,500) — dramatically cheaper than dinner.
- **Himeji Castle** — Japan's finest original castle; ⚠️ the non-resident adult fee was raised to **¥2,500** (in effect since March 2026) with **under-18s free** — verify the current fee. ~30 min by Sanyo Shinkansen from Shin-Osaka.
- **Hiroshima** — Peace Memorial Park & A-Bomb Dome (free, open-air); Museum ¥200. Sobering and a must-see.
- **Miyajima** — the "floating" Itsukushima torii. Shrine ¥300, ferry ¥200 + ¥100 island visitor tax. **Check the tide table** — high tide for the floating shot, low tide to walk out to the gate.

> **The key western rail deal:** the tourist-only **JR Kansai-Hiroshima Area Pass** (5-day, ~¥17,000/₹10,030) covers Shin-Osaka↔Hiroshima Sanyo Shinkansen + Kobe + Himeji + the JR Miyajima ferry. One Hiroshima round trip nearly pays for it. Far better value than the nationwide JR Pass for a Kansai-only trip. (Prices change — verify.)

### Beyond the Golden Route (great October add-ons)
- **Central Japan / "New Golden Route":** Kanazawa (Kenroku-en garden, geisha teahouses, ~2h30 from Tokyo), Shirakawa-go (UNESCO farmhouses), Takayama, and the **Tateyama-Kurobe Alpine Route** — whose high sections (Murodo) hit **peak foliage in early-to-mid October**, one of the country's best autumn sights.
- **Hokkaido & Okinawa:** treat as *separate fly-in legs*, not shinkansen add-ons. In **October, Hokkaido's Daisetsuzan is Japan's earliest foliage** (first half of the month) and it has the lowest typhoon risk; Okinawa = tropical beaches. Fly (Tokyo→Sapporo ~1.5h; the shinkansen still doesn't reach Sapporo). The JR Pass does *not* help here.

## 6. Food: exploring Japanese cuisine

You didn't fly to Japan to play it safe at dinner. This is one of the world's great eating countries, and the joy is in *exploring* it — grazing markets, decoding ticket-machine ramen shops, counting your plates at conveyor-belt sushi, and hunting down each city's signature dish. Come hungry and curious.

**October is a delicious month to do it.** Autumn is *shokuyoku no aki* — "the season of appetite." Look out for **matsutake mushrooms**, **sanma** (Pacific saury, salt-grilled with grated daikon), **chestnuts**, **roasted sweet potato** (*yaki-imo*), **new-crop rice**, and **hiyaoroshi** autumn sake — plus limited-edition autumn Kit Kats (sweet potato, Mont Blanc) and momiji-maple sweets (more seasonal foods in Section 7).

### 🍜 The regional food map — eat the city's specialty
| City / region | Don't leave without eating | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Tokyo** | Edomae sushi, shoyu ramen, tempura, *monjayaki* | Tsukiji Outer Market (sushi by 8–9 AM); Tsukishima's Monja Street |
| **Osaka** ("Japan's kitchen") | Takoyaki, okonomiyaki, kushikatsu | Dotonbori & Kuromon — Wanaka/Aizuya (takoyaki), Mizuno/Chibo (okonomiyaki), Daruma (kushikatsu — *no double-dipping the sauce!*) |
| **Kyoto** | Kaiseki, yudofu (tofu hot pot), matcha sweets, soba | Nishiki Market; Honke Owariya (soba, 500+ yrs); Nakamura Tokichi (matcha) |
| **Fukuoka / Hakata** | Tonkotsu ramen at open-air *yatai* stalls, motsunabe | Nakasu island stalls |
| **Sapporo (Hokkaido)** | Miso ramen, soup curry, uni & crab, Genghis Khan (grilled lamb) | Otaru markets; Uni Murakami |
| **Nagoya** | Hitsumabushi (grilled eel three ways), misokatsu, tebasaki wings | Yabaton |
| **Hiroshima** | Okonomiyaki (Hiroshima-style — layered, with noodles) | — |
| **Kobe** | A5 wagyu teppanyaki (do it at *lunch* to save big) | Steakland, from ¥3,500 |

### 🍽️ Only-in-Japan ways to eat (try them all)
- **Conveyor-belt (kaiten) sushi** — Sushiro / Kura / Hama; order on a touch-screen, an express "train" delivers your plates, and you **pay by counting the stacked plates** (¥110–150/plate). Rule: take one plate at a time, and never put one back on the belt.
- **Ticket-machine ramen shops** — insert cash at the machine by the door, press the (photo) button, hand the printed ticket to the chef, sit. Order a *kaedama* (noodle refill) at tonkotsu shops. Slurping is a compliment.
- **Depachika** — the food halls in department-store basements (Isetan Shinjuku B1 is the benchmark): 200+ stalls, free samples, jewel-like bentos, and premium items discounted in the early evening. Assemble a picnic.
- **Themed & animal cafes** — Akihabara is ground zero: maid cafes, cat/hedgehog cafes, and character cafes (Kirby, Pokémon). Book the popular ones ahead.
- **Grill-your-own** — *yakiniku* (Japanese BBQ), *shabu-shabu* (swish paper-thin beef in dashi), *sukiyaki*, and *teppanyaki*; often available as *tabehodai* (all-you-can-eat) / *nomihodai* (all-you-can-drink) for a set time.

### 🏪 Konbini & vending adventures (cheaper than you'd believe, better than you'd expect)
Run a **konbini taste-off** across the big three: **7-Eleven** (the cult *tamago sando* egg sandwich, onigiri), **Lawson** (Uchi Café desserts, oden), and **FamilyMart** (FamiChiki hot fried chicken). Then the **vending machines** — from late October they switch to "warm mode" (**red label = hot**): hot canned coffee (Boss, Georgia), corn soup, sweet *amazake*, roasted *hojicha* tea. ¥100–160 each, and a hot can doubles as a hand-warmer.

### 🥢 Do it like a local
- **Lunch is the value hack:** high-end sushi and kaiseki cost a fraction at lunch (e.g. a ¥3,000–4,000 kaiseki set vs ¥15,000 at dinner). Gyudon (beef-bowl) chains — Yoshinoya, Sukiya, Matsuya — feed you well for under ¥500.
- **Food tours & cooking classes** are the fast track to exploring: a Tsukiji or Shinjuku (Omoide Yokocho / Golden Gai) night food tour (~¥6,000–10,000), or a hands-on sushi / ramen / gyoza cooking class (~¥6,000–16,500).
- **Graze the markets:** Nishiki (Kyoto), Kuromon (Osaka — go before 11 AM), Tsukiji Outer & Ameyoko (Tokyo). Don't walk-and-eat; stand aside by the stall. Carry cash — many are cash-only.
- **Ordering made easy:** point at the plastic food models (*sampuru*) in the window, or say *"Kore o kudasai"* (this one, please). There's **no tipping**, ever.

### 😈 The brave-eater bucket list
Go all in on the flavours you can only get here: **uni** (sea urchin — creamy, sweet-briny), **natto** (sticky fermented soybeans, a breakfast rite of passage), **fugu** (pufferfish — prepared only by licensed chefs, so it's safe; a winter and Osaka specialty at Guenpin or Zuboraya), **basashi** (raw horse sashimi — Kumamoto's pride), and **shirako** (cod milt, a creamy autumn/winter delicacy). Not ready yet? Start with the crowd-pleasers — tonkatsu, katsu curry, gyoza, tempura, yakitori, unagi, and any *donburi* rice bowl — then work your way up the ladder.

## 7. Your October trip: autumn colour, festivals and packing

Good news: **October is one of the very best months to visit Japan** — mild, dry, low-humidity, and the shoulder between summer heat and the November foliage crush. Here's how to make the most of *your* month.

### 🍁 The autumn-foliage nuance (read this before you plan)
Autumn colour (*koyo*) sweeps across Japan **north→south and high→low**, so in October it peaks **only in the north and up high** — the classic red-maple images of Kyoto and Tokyo are a **mid-to-late-November** phenomenon and those cities will still be **green** while you're there.

**Peaking IN October — go here for colour:**
- **Hokkaido — Daisetsuzan** (Japan's earliest): peaks late Sept–early Oct; Sounkyo / Jozankei ~early–mid Oct. Bonus: the lowest typhoon risk in the country.
- **Japanese Alps — Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route** (Murodo peaks ~late Sept–early Oct) and **Kamikochi** (maples plus golden larch, mid–late Oct). The best all-rounder for a mid-to-late-October trip.
- **Nikko** (easy day/overnight from Tokyo) — Lake Chuzenji, Kegon Falls and the Irohazaka switchbacks peak mid–late Oct. The lowest-effort colour add-on.
- **Tohoku** — Oirase Gorge and Lake Towada: dramatic and uncrowded, early–mid Oct.

**Still green in October** (enjoy them for temples, food and festivals, not leaves): **Kyoto, Tokyo, Osaka, Nara, Hakone, Miyajima.** Bonus: **Mt Fuji usually gets its first snow cap in early October** — a gorgeous shot even before the maples turn.

> **Chase-colour tip:** structure the trip **north/high first, cities/south later**, or simply bolt a Nikko / Alps / Hokkaido leg onto the Golden Route (see the October add-ons in Section 2). Check live *koyo* reports (japan-guide, JMA) ~2 weeks out — a hot summer can push peaks later.

### 🎌 October festivals & events (with dates)
- **Nagasaki Kunchi** — Oct 7–9 (400-year-old festival; Chinese dragon dances, Kyushu)
- **Takayama Autumn Festival** — Oct 9–10 (one of Japan's three great float festivals; book lodging months ahead)
- **Kawagoe Festival** — Oct 17–18, 2026 (huge Edo-style floats; easy day trip from Tokyo)
- **Jidai Matsuri** (Kyoto) — Oct 22 (a procession spanning 1,000 years of costume) — and, the same evening, the dramatic **Kurama Fire Festival** in the hills north of Kyoto
- **Also:** Nabana no Sato winter illuminations open ~Oct 18 (near Nagoya); Tokyo's Nihonbashi-Kyobashi Matsuri (late Oct); and the sumo **autumn regional tour** (*jungyo*, ~Oct 6–25) — a rarer, intimate way to see the wrestlers
- **Halloween — Oct 31:** central **Shibuya has banned street gatherings** (public-drinking ban 6 PM–5 AM around the Scramble Crossing). The fun has moved to theme parks (Disney, USJ) and ticketed events — families should skip central Shibuya that night.

> ⚠️ **Mind the long weekend:** **Sports Day is Monday, Oct 12, 2026**, making **Oct 10–12** a domestic long weekend that overlaps the Takayama festival. Expect packed shinkansen, crowded foliage hotspots and pricier hotels. Book early, or avoid intercity travel on those three days.

### 🌦️ Weather, typhoons & crowds
- **Temperatures:** Tokyo/Kyoto/Osaka ~23–28°C in early October, easing to ~19–20°C late in the month with cooler nights; Hokkaido and the Alps get genuinely cold (near-freezing at altitude by month-end).
- **Typhoon tail:** October is the tail of typhoon season (2–3 storms may approach). Keep **buffer days**, book **refundable** flights/hotels, front-load your must-do outdoor plans, and keep an indoor rain-day option (museums, food halls). Risk drops noticeably in the second half of the month; Hokkaido is least affected.
- **Crowds & prices:** early October is genuine shoulder-season value; late October builds as the colour marches south — but Kyoto is still far cheaper than its mid-November foliage peak.

### 🧳 What to pack for October
| Layer | Bring |
|---|---|
| Base / mid | T-shirts + long-sleeves + a light sweater or cardigan (warm days, cool evenings) |
| Outer | A mid-weight jacket or a light trench/windbreaker for evenings |
| Rain | A packable rain jacket + a compact umbrella (typhoon tail, especially early Oct) |
| Warm (if going north/high) | Light down + beanie + gloves for Hokkaido / Tateyama / Kamikochi, or early mornings late in the month |
| Feet | Comfortable walking shoes — expect a lot of temples, festivals and foliage trails |

## 10. Deals and passes: JR Pass, IC cards and city passes

### Is the JR Pass worth it? Usually NO for a first trip.

After the 2023 hike, the nationwide **7-day JR Pass is ¥50,000 (~₹29,400)** — and it no longer auto-pays-off.

| JR Pass (Ordinary) | Price (verify) | ≈ ₹ |
|---|---|---|
| 7-day | ¥50,000 | ~₹29,400 |
| 14-day | ¥80,000 | ~₹47,200 |
| 21-day | ¥100,000 | ~₹59,000 |

**The math (and a crucial catch):** a **non-reserved** Tokyo–Kyoto round trip is only ~¥26,600–27,700 (reserved on the Nozomi is dearer, ~¥28,340 RT). Either way, on a plain Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka loop you **LOSE money** buying the pass.

It only starts to break even on something like **Tokyo → Hiroshima and back within the week** — **but remember a pass holder CANNOT ride the Nozomi**, so you must price the break-even on the slower **Hikari/Sakura** the pass actually allows (and add ~15–25 min per long leg). On the allowed trains, a Tokyo + Kyoto + Hiroshima week can total roughly ¥55,000–62,000 of rides — only then does the pass edge ahead.

For most focused first trips, use **regional passes** instead: **Kansai-Hiroshima ~¥17,000**; **JR Takayama-Hokuriku** for the Central-Japan loop; **JR East Tohoku Area Pass ~¥30,000** (5-day) for the north-east. (Products and prices change — verify the exact pass and fare for your route.)

> ⚠️ **Price note (verify):** an increase to the overseas-agency price of the 7-day pass has been reported (to ~¥53,000 from October 2026), while the official Japan Rail Pass website (japanrailpass.net) has been quoted as holding lower. Compare both at the time you buy.

### IC cards — your single most useful buy (explained once here; referenced elsewhere)

Tap one card for all trains, subways, buses AND konbini/vending machines nationwide (Suica/Pasmo in Tokyo, ICOCA in Kansai — all cross-compatible).

- **Welcome Suica** (tourist card): ¥1,500 loaded, **no deposit**, valid 28 days, but leftover balance is **non-refundable** — spend it down. Buy at airport JR counters/machines.
- **Standard Suica/ICOCA:** ¥500 refundable deposit; ICOCA is ¥2,000 (¥1,500 usable). Refundable at JR machines.
- **Mobile:** iPhone users can add Suica to Apple Wallet and top up with an international card — the queue-free option. **Mobile Suica for tourists is iPhone-only**, so Indian **Android** users should grab a physical Welcome Suica at the airport.

### City passes worth it (only on paid-attraction/heavy-transit days)

| Pass | Price (verify) | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Tokyo Subway Ticket** (24/48/72h) | ¥800 / ¥1,200 / ¥1,500 | Subway-heavy sightseeing (not JR) |
| **Kyoto Subway & Bus 1-Day** | ¥1,100 | Arashiyama + Kinkaku-ji + Gion in a day |
| **Osaka Amazing Pass** (1/2-day) | ¥3,500 / ¥5,000 | Castle + Umeda Sky + river cruise + metro |

| **Kansai-Hiroshima Area Pass** (5-day) | ~¥17,000 | Osaka + Kobe + Himeji + Hiroshima + Miyajima ferry |

### Money-saving tips
- **Overnight highway buses** (Willer, Tokyo↔Kyoto from ¥3,500) save the fare *and* a hotel night.
- Buy shinkansen legs via **smartEX** or Klook (pay with an India-issued Visa for ~10% off on Klook).
- **Cheap eats:** gyudon ~¥450, kaiten sushi ¥110/plate, konbini bento ¥400–700. Supermarkets slash bento 30–50% in the last 1–2 hours.
- **Capsule hotels** ¥2,000–5,000; **business hotels** (APA, Toyoko Inn) ¥7,000–15,000.

## 11. Stamps! The eki and goshuin mini-guide

Japan has three overlapping stamp traditions that turn your trip into a treasure hunt — one of the best cheap souvenirs an Indian traveller can bring home. **The golden rule: keep the playful/secular stamps and the sacred stamps in two separate books — never mix them.**

### 1. Eki stamps (station stamps) — FREE

Almost every train station keeps a self-serve rubber stamp with a unique local design — a sumo wrestler at Ryogoku, a geisha at Gion-Shijo, the floating torii at Miyajimaguchi. They sit near the ticket gates or the station master's window. **The ritual:** test-press on scrap paper first (old pads print faint), press firmly, and write the station name + date beside it.

*Digital version:* the free **EKITAG** app collects stamps by scanning NFC/QR at many stations — a great backup when a pad is dried out.

### 2. Goshuin — sacred temple/shrine calligraphy stamps

A **goshuin** is hand-brushed for you on the spot: the name in calligraphy, the date, and a vermilion seal — a small work of art *and* a spiritual record. It is **NOT a stamp rally.**

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Buy the book first** | A **goshuincho** (accordion-fold washi book), ¥1,000–3,000 (~₹590–1,770). Staff may turn you away if you hand over a normal notebook. |
| **Cost per stamp** | ¥300–500 (~₹175–295); ¥500–1,000 for limited/seasonal editions |
| **CASH ONLY, exact change** | Carry 100- and 500-yen coins |
| **Pray FIRST** | The stamp is proof of worship, not an entry ticket |
| **Never mix sacred & secular** | Some strict temples won't stamp a book that has shrine seals — carry two goshuincho if doing lots of both |

Beginner-friendly spots: Senso-ji, Meiji Jingu (Tokyo); Kiyomizu-dera, Yasaka-jinja (Kyoto). Buy a beautiful goshuincho at Kyukyodo (est. 1663) or the temple itself. In autumn, many shrines issue **special seasonal (momiji) goshuin** — a lovely October keepsake.

### 3. Stamp rallies (with prizes)
- **JR East × Pokémon stamp rallies** run most years (often in summer, sometimes seasonal editions) — a free stamp book, collect across dozens of stations, win prizes. Check JR East's site for the current edition.
- **100 Famous Castles Rally** (no deadline) — grab castle stamps where you already are (Himeji, Matsumoto, Osaka).

**Notebook for eki/rally stamps:** a passport-size book from Kinokuniya (~¥825/₹490) — pick **thick pages** so ink doesn't bleed.

## 12. Money and budget: cash, cards and a realistic INR budget

### The cash reality

Japan is increasingly cashless, but **a large share of spend is still cash** — essential at shrines, small ramen shops, markets, coin lockers, and rural areas. Carry **¥20,000–30,000 (~₹12,000–18,000)** per person for a week; top up cheaply in Japan rather than over-loading cash in India. (There is no tipping — see Section 14.)

- **Cards & payments:** Visa & Mastercard are widest in cities. **RuPay is not accepted in Japan** — carry a Visa/Mastercard. UPI is *not yet* live (a bilateral trial is underway), so don't rely on it.
- **Forex markup — quantify the saving:** a regular Indian credit/debit card typically adds **~3.5% forex markup (plus GST on that)**. A **zero-markup card (Niyo, Scapia, Fi)** removes it — on ₹1 lakh of card spend that's **~₹3,500+ saved**, which is why it's the single best money move for this trip.
- **ATMs:** **7-Eleven (Seven Bank)** and **Japan Post** reliably take Indian Visa/Mastercard 24/7 with English menus — most bank ATMs reject foreign cards. Before flying, ask your Indian bank to *enable international ATM withdrawals* and confirm a **4-digit PIN** (6-digit PINs are rejected).
- **Golden rule:** when an ATM/terminal asks "charge in JPY or INR?" → **always choose JPY** (choosing INR = dynamic currency conversion, 3–8% worse).
- **Best combo for an Indian:** a zero-markup card + ¥20–30k cash from 7-Eleven + a Suica (Section 10).

### Realistic ~10-day budget (per person, ex-India)

| Item | Budget | Mid-range |
|---|---|---|
| Flights (round-trip) | ₹45,000 | ₹75,000 |
| Visa (all-in) | ₹1,300 | ₹1,300 |

| Accommodation (9 nights) | ₹18,000 (capsule/hostel ~₹2,000/night) | ₹54,000 (hotel ~₹6,000/night) |
| Rail/transport | ₹15,000 (IC + regional pass) | ₹20,000 |

### Getting around
- **IC card first** (Section 10) — tap trains/buses/konbini.

Self-drive only really comes up for **Hokkaido, Okinawa or the Shirakawa-go/Takayama countryside** — arrange the JAF translation in advance and don't waste money on an IDP that won't be accepted.

## 14. Etiquette, onsen, toilets and tap water

The unwritten rules matter more here than almost anywhere — and they're the biggest adjustment coming from India.

**DO:**
- Keep **silence on trains** — phones on "manner mode," no calls. (This is the #1 culture gap.)
- Take **shoes off** at ryokan, temples, homes, some restaurants (watch for the genkan step).
- **Wash thoroughly before** entering an onsen; enter naked; the small towel never touches the water.
- **Queue neatly**, stand on the correct escalator side (left in Tokyo, right in Osaka).
- Carry a **small bag for your trash** — public bins are rare; hold it till a konbini/station.
- Bow with a small head nod; say **"Sumimasen"** (excuse me/sorry — the most useful word).

**DON'T:**
- **Never tip** — it confuses or mildly offends. (This is the one canonical mention: no tipping anywhere in Japan, ever.)
- Don't eat while walking; step aside near the stall.
- Don't stand chopsticks upright in rice or pass food chopstick-to-chopstick (both are funeral rites).
- Don't photograph geiko in Gion's private lanes (**¥10,000 fine**).
- Don't haggle — prices are fixed.

### 🚰 Tap water & 🚽 toilets (two big India-traveller questions)
- **Tap water is potable nationwide** — safe to drink straight from the tap and to refill a bottle. Skip bottled water to save money; carry a refillable bottle.
- **Washlet toilets are everywhere** — heated seats and a **water/bidet spray with adjustable pressure**, which most Indian travellers find far more comfortable than paper-only Western toilets. Buttons are usually pictogram-labelled (look for "spray/bidet" and "stop"). You'll find washlets in hotels, malls, department stores, konbini and stations.
- **Occasional squat toilets** appear at older stations, some temples and parks — cubicles are usually marked with a Western/washlet option too; pick that stall.

> **Onsen & tattoos (and mehndi):** many baths refuse visible tattoos (an old yakuza association). Fixes: cover small ink with waterproof **seals** (Don Quijote, ~¥200–800), pick a **tattoo-friendly town** (Kinosaki — all 7 baths welcome tattoos; Beppu; parts of Hokkaido), or — best for modesty, mixed families, tattoos *and* fresh mehndi alike — book a **kashikiri (private) bath or a room with an in-room rotenburo.** Public baths are gender-separated and require full nudity, so a couple/family can only bathe *together* in a private bath. *(An onsen ryokan on a chilly October night is one of the trip's great pleasures — build one in.)*

## 15. Health, emergencies, pre-departure checklist and cheat sheet

### 🏥 Pharmacies, medicines & women's essentials
- **Drugstore chains** (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Welcia, Sundrug, Cosmos, plus Don Quijote) are everywhere and stock painkillers, cold remedies, plasters, ORS-type drinks, motion-sickness tablets and skincare. Staff often have a translation tablet.
- **Menstrual supplies:** **sanitary pads are widely available** in any drugstore/konbini; **tampons are stocked but far less common** (look in larger drugstores), and **menstrual cups are rare** — bring your preferred product from India if you rely on tampons/cups.
- **Prescription meds:** carry max ~1-month supply in original packaging with a doctor's letter. ⚠️ **Pseudoephedrine and codeine — common in Indian cold/cough medicines — are banned**; check labels before packing. For larger quantities or controlled meds, a **Yakkan Shoumei** import certificate may be required — check in advance.

### 🆘 Emergency & consular help for Indians
- **Emergency numbers:** **110 Police · 119 Fire/Ambulance · 118 Coast Guard.** JNTO Visitor Hotline **050-3816-2787** (24/7, English).
- **Embassy of India, Tokyo:** 2-2-11 Kudan-Minami, Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo 102-0074; main line **+81-3-3262-2391**. Covers eastern Japan.
- **Consulate General of India, Osaka-Kobe:** Semba I.S. Building, 1-5-8 Kyutaro-machi, Chuo-ku, Osaka 541-0056; main line **+81-6-6261-7299**. Covers western Japan (Osaka/Kansai/Hiroshima region).
- For a **lost passport**, file a police report first, then contact the mission for an emergency certificate/replacement. **Confirm the current 24/7 emergency contact number on the mission's official website before you travel** and save it offline.
