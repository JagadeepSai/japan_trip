# PART 1 — TOKYO (in the city)

For energetic young-adult travellers who want experiences *and* the classic must-sees.
Prices are in **¥** with an approx **₹** conversion at **¥100 ≈ ₹58** (so ¥1,000 ≈ ₹580).
Hours/prices shift with season and demand — treat as "opening-ish" and reconfirm on the official site.

## Book these in ADVANCE (they sell out / are reservation-only)
- **Ghibli Museum, Mitaka** — reservation-only; tickets drop 10th of each month (10:00 JST) for the *next* month and vanish in minutes. Name must match passport.
- **Shibuya Sky** — the sunset slot sells out in ~2 minutes; tickets release ~2 weeks ahead at 00:00 JST.
- **teamLab Borderless** & **teamLab Planets** — timed entry, book days ahead.
- **Tokyo DisneySea / Disneyland** — date-priced; DisneySea's new **Fantasy Springs** area needs paid Premier Access or a standby pass.
- **Warner Bros. Studio Tour Tokyo (Harry Potter)** — timed slots, routinely sold out weeks out.
- **Yomiuri Giants playoff (Climax Series) baseball** in October — instant sell-outs.

## 1. Iconic must-sees

1. **Shibuya Scramble Crossing** — *Shibuya.* The world's busiest pedestrian crossing; time it with the lights, then shoot it from above. **Free** (best free overhead view: Mag's Park rooftop or the Shibuya Tsutaya Starbucks window). *What it's for:* the definitive Tokyo neon-chaos moment.
2. **Shibuya Sky** — *Shibuya (Scramble Square, 47F + open-air roof, 229m).* Adult **¥2,500 advance / ¥3,000 door**; **sunset "premium" ¥3,100 advance / ¥3,600** (priority + drink). Open ~10:00–22:30 (last entry ~21:20). *What it's for:* best open-air 360° sunset deck in the city — book the golden-hour slot early.
3. **Senso-ji Temple & Nakamise-dori** — *Asakusa.* Tokyo's oldest temple (founded 645) + the Kaminarimon lantern gate and a souvenir/snack shopping street. **Free**, grounds ~always open (main hall 06:00–17:00). *What it's for:* old-Tokyo temple atmosphere; go by 08:00 to beat crowds.
4. **Meiji Jingu** — *Harajuku / Yoyogi.* Serene Shinto shrine in a 100,000-tree forest honouring Emperor Meiji. **Free** (Inner Garden ¥500 ≈ ₹290; Treasure Museum ~¥1,000 ≈ ₹580). Sunrise–sunset. *What it's for:* instant calm two minutes from Harajuku's madness; you may catch a wedding procession.
5. **Tokyo Skytree** — *Sumida / Oshiage (634m, tallest tower in Japan).* Tembo Deck (350m) **from ¥1,800 advance** (≈ ₹1,045); +Tembo Galleria (450m) combo **from ¥3,000** (≈ ₹1,740). ~10:00–21:00. *What it's for:* the highest, most panoramic paid view; pair with Solamachi mall below.
6. **Tokyo Tower** — *Minato (333m, the red Eiffel-style icon).* Main Deck (150m) **¥1,500** (≈ ₹870); Top Deck Tour (250m) **¥3,300 online / ¥3,500 counter** (≈ ₹1,915). ~09:00–22:30. *What it's for:* the nostalgic retro-Tokyo landmark, best lit up at night.
7. **Imperial Palace East Gardens** — *Chiyoda.* The Emperor's residence grounds; you can walk the East Gardens and Nijubashi bridge viewpoint. **Free** (gardens closed Mon & Fri). *What it's for:* moats, stone ramparts and green calm in the city core; book the free interior tour separately.
8. **teamLab Borderless** — *Azabudai Hills, Minato.* Boundary-less immersive digital-art museum where artworks wander between rooms. **¥3,600–¥5,600** adults (≈ ₹2,090–₹3,250, date-priced), 10:00–21:00. Book ahead. *What it's for:* the flagship "get lost inside the art" experience.
9. **teamLab Planets** — *Toyosu.* Wade barefoot through water rooms and a mirrored infinity garden. **¥3,200** adult (≈ ₹1,855), child ¥800 (≈ ₹465), 09:00–22:00 (last entry 21:00). Open through **end of 2027**. *What it's for:* the wetter, more physical, most Instagram-viral teamLab — wear shorts.

## 2. Neighbourhoods to explore (and their vibe)

10. **Shibuya** — youth-culture ground zero: the crossing, Center-Gai, 109 fashion, Miyashita Park rooftop, endless bars. Loud, neon, always-on.
11. **Shinjuku** — a city within the city: skyscrapers + free gov't observatory west, Kabukicho nightlife + Golden Gai + Omoide Yokocho east, Japan's busiest station in between. Overwhelming and electric.
12. **Harajuku** — **Takeshita-dori** for teen kawaii/street fashion, crepes and purikura; **Omotesando** for tree-lined luxury flagships; **Ura-Harajuku (Cat Street)** for streetwear. Playful and photogenic.
13. **Asakusa** — old-Tokyo temple town: Senso-ji, rickshaws, Nakamise crafts, retro Hoppy-dori drinking street. Traditional and touristy in a good way.
14. **Akihabara** — "Electric Town": anime, manga, retro games, electronics, maid cafes, multi-floor arcades. Otaku heaven.
15. **Ginza** — Tokyo's polished luxury quarter: flagship department stores, art galleries, sushi counters; car-free "pedestrian paradise" on weekend afternoons. Grown-up and glossy.
16. **Ueno** — museum row (National Museum, science, art), Ueno Park, the zoo, and the rowdy **Ameyoko** market. Cultural + gritty market energy.
17. **Nakameguro** — canal-side cool: designer boutiques, third-wave coffee, wine bars along the Meguro River (peak cherry blossoms). Grown-up hipster.
18. **Shimokitazawa** — bohemian thrift, indie record shops, tiny theatres and live music. Artsy, low-rise, young and creative.
19. **Koenji** — Shimokita's punkier, cheaper cousin: vintage, dive izakayas, live houses, home of the summer Awa-Odori dance. Underground and local.
20. **Odaiba** — man-made bay island: seaside park, Rainbow Bridge views, malls, Joypolis, teamLab Planets nearby, life-size Gundam statue. Futuristic day-out.
21. **Roppongi** — art + nightlife: Mori Art Museum / Tokyo City View, expat bars and clubs. Upscale after dark.
22. **Yanaka** — a rare survivor of the WWII bombing: wooden houses, temple lanes, the **Yanaka Ginza** shopping street and resident cats. Nostalgic and slow.

## 3. Culture & traditional experiences

23. **Grand Sumo / Ryogoku Kokugikan** — *Ryogoku.* Tokyo tournaments run **Jan, May & Sep** (15 days); tickets from ~¥3,800 (≈ ₹2,205). In **October** there's no Tokyo *basho*, but the **Autumn Jungyo** touring exhibition (~Oct 6–25) lets you see wrestlers train and interact up close — check stops. Off-season, book a **morning stable (heya) practice viewing**. *What it's for:* Japan's national sport, live.
24. **Tea ceremony in kimono** — *Asakusa (e.g. Maikoya).* Learn the matcha ritual dressed in kimono, ~¥6,000–¥9,000 (≈ ₹3,480–₹5,220). *What it's for:* a hands-on slice of wa (harmony) culture.
25. **Kimono / yukata rental** — *Asakusa.* Half-day rental with dressing ~¥3,000–¥5,000 (≈ ₹1,740–₹2,900); stroll Senso-ji in it. *What it's for:* the classic dress-up-and-wander photo day.
26. **Samurai Ninja Museum with Experience** — *Asakusa.* Wear armour, throw shuriken, try sword-drawing. Basic entry **¥3,000** (≈ ₹1,740); ninja training +entry ¥6,000, sword-cutting (tameshigiri) ¥12,000 (≈ ₹6,960). *What it's for:* interactive samurai/ninja fun, family-friendly.
27. **Calligraphy / origami / furoshiki class** — *various (Asakusa, Ginza).* Short guided workshops, ~¥2,000–¥4,000 (≈ ₹1,160–₹2,320). *What it's for:* a quiet, crafty, made-it-myself souvenir.
28. **Samurai Restaurant** — *Shinjuku (Kabukicho).* The over-the-top neon dinner show that replaced the Robot Restaurant in the same venue — dancers, drums, giant robots, lasers. ~¥8,000+ (≈ ₹4,640+). *What it's for:* peak "only-in-Tokyo" kitsch spectacle.
29. **Traditional gardens** — *Rikugien (Komagome), Koishikawa Korakuen (Bunkyo), Hama-rikyu (Chuo).* Landscaped Edo strolling gardens; ~¥300–¥400 each (≈ ₹175–₹230). A **Garden Pass (~¥600 ≈ ₹350)** covers nine gardens — worth it for 3+. *What it's for:* teahouses, ponds and (Nov–Dec) fiery maple foliage.

## 4. Food & drink experiences

30. **Tsukiji Outer Market** — *Tsukiji.* Grazing paradise: fresh sushi, tamagoyaki skewers, grilled scallops, uni, strawberry daifuku. Best ~07:00–13:00 (many stalls close early afternoon; quiet Sun/Wed). *What it's for:* Tokyo's ultimate street-food breakfast crawl.
31. **Toyosu Market tuna auction** — *Toyosu.* The relocated wholesale market; watch the pre-dawn tuna auction from viewing decks (early booking) or just eat legendary sushi-for-breakfast. *What it's for:* the "real" fish-market spectacle Tsukiji used to host.
32. **Depachika (department-store food halls)** — *Ginza (Mitsukoshi), Shinjuku (Isetan).* Basement floors of jewel-like bento, wagashi, patisserie and free samples. **Free** to browse. *What it's for:* the most beautiful edible window-shopping on earth.
33. **Omoide Yokocho ("Memory Lane / Piss Alley")** — *Shinjuku west.* Smoky WWII-era alley of tiny yakitori & motsu counters. Skewers ~¥150–¥400 each. *What it's for:* atmospheric, elbow-to-elbow retro drinking-and-grilling.
34. **Conveyor-belt sushi (kaiten-zushi)** — *chains like Uobei/Genki/Sushiro, citywide.* Touch-screen order, plates from ~¥120 (≈ ₹70). *What it's for:* cheap, fast, fun sushi with zero language barrier.
35. **Ramen crawl** — *Tokyo Ramen Street (Tokyo Station B1)* packs ~8 famous shops under one roof; bowls ~¥900–¥1,300 (≈ ₹520–₹755). *What it's for:* sampling shoyu/miso/tonkotsu masters without hopping the city.
36. **Michelin ramen on a budget** — *e.g. Nakiryu (Otsuka), Tsuta (Yoshicho).* Michelin-rated bowls ~¥1,000–¥2,500 (≈ ₹580–₹1,450); expect queues. *What it's for:* a Michelin meal for pocket change — the great Tokyo lunch hack.
37. **Themed & character cafes** — *Pokémon Cafe (Nihonbashi), Kirby Cafe (Skytree), Shirokuma-inspired & animal cafes.* Reservation often required; sets ~¥1,500–¥3,000 (≈ ₹870–₹1,740). *What it's for:* cute, collectible, over-designed food experiences.
38. **Tsukiji cooking / market food tour** — *Tsukiji.* Guided tasting + a class where you fillet fish and cook Japanese basics. ~¥8,000–¥15,000 (≈ ₹4,640–₹8,700). *What it's for:* eat *and* learn from a local.
39. **Sake & Japanese-whisky tasting** — *Shinjuku / Ginza tasting bars, or a brewery/distillery-style flight bar.* Flights ~¥1,500–¥3,500 (≈ ₹870–₹2,030). *What it's for:* structured intro to nihonshu and Japan's world-beating whiskies.

## 5. Nightlife & entertainment

40. **WOMB** — *Shibuya.* Legendary multi-floor club, monster sound system, techno/house, big international DJs. Cover ~¥3,000–¥5,000 (≈ ₹1,740–₹2,900), doors ~23:00–05:00. *What it's for:* Tokyo's iconic big-room clubbing.
41. **ZeroTokyo / Kabukicho Tower** & **ageHa (Shin-Kiba)** — *Shinjuku / bayfront.* Huge event-driven venues (ageHa has an outdoor poolside floor). Cover ~¥3,000–¥5,000. *What it's for:* mega-club EDM/dance nights (check event calendars — ageHa runs specific event dates).
42. **Golden Gai** — *Shinjuku.* ~200 shoebox theme bars (horror, jazz vinyl, punk, wrestling), 4–8 seats each; expect a ~¥500–¥1,500 seating/cover charge (≈ ₹290–₹870). *What it's for:* bar-hopping tiny worlds and chatting with strangers.
43. **Karaoke till dawn** — *Karaoke-kan, Big Echo, Karaoke no Tetsujin, citywide.* Rooms ~¥1,000–¥2,500/hr (≈ ₹580–₹1,450), often "free-time" all-night deals; drink bars included. *What it's for:* the essential private-room Tokyo night-ender.
44. **Live houses** — *Shimokitazawa & Koenji.* Intimate indie/rock/punk venues; entry ~¥2,500–¥3,500 (≈ ₹1,450–₹2,030) usually +1 drink. *What it's for:* discovering Tokyo's underground music scene.
45. **Jazz** — *Blue Note Tokyo (Aoyama)* for name acts (~¥7,000+ ≈ ₹4,060+); *Shinjuku Pit Inn* for local legends (from ~¥1,500 ≈ ₹870). *What it's for:* Tokyo's deep, serious jazz culture.
46. **Standing bars (tachinomi) & yokocho** — *Ebisu Yokocho, Ura-Ginza, Nonbei Yokocho (Shibuya).* Cheap drinks/small plates elbow-to-elbow with salarymen. *What it's for:* fast, social, wallet-friendly local drinking.

## 6. Quirky / only-in-Tokyo

47. **Street go-karting** — *Shibuya / Akihabara / Asakusa (Street Kart, costumes provided).* ~¥15,000–¥20,000 (≈ ₹8,700–₹11,600). **Requires an International Driving Permit** (Geneva-convention) — arrange before you fly. *What it's for:* real-life kart racing past the neon (Mario costumes no longer official but the thrill remains).
48. **Purikura** — *Harajuku (Takeshita-dori), Shibuya arcades.* Glammed-up photo booths that enlarge eyes and add stickers; ~¥400–¥600 (≈ ₹230–₹350). *What it's for:* the ultimate cutesy group souvenir.
49. **Mega arcades** — *Namco Akihabara, GiGO Akihabara/Shibuya, Taito Station.* Rhythm games, claw machines, retro cabinets; ~¥100/play. *What it's for:* hours of pure Japanese gaming chaos.
50. **Gachapon capsule-toy halls** — *Akihabara Gachapon Kaikan / Gashapon Bandai Odaiba.* Hundreds of machines; ~¥300–¥500 a spin (≈ ₹175–₹290). *What it's for:* addictive random collectible fun.
51. **Animal cafes** — *Harajuku/Akihabara.* Hedgehog, owl, capybara, mini-pig, otter and cat cafes; ~¥1,000–¥2,500 (≈ ₹580–₹1,450) incl. a drink/time slot. *What it's for:* petting exotic cuties (pick well-reviewed, ethical spots).
52. **Maid cafes** — *Akihabara (Maidreamin, @home cafe).* Costumed servers, cutesy chants, omurice art; entry + set ~¥1,500–¥3,000 (≈ ₹870–₹1,740). *What it's for:* the classic Akiba pop-culture rabbit hole.
53. **Tokyo Joypolis** — *Odaiba (Sega indoor amusement park).* VR rides, a indoor coaster and arcade over 3 floors. Passport ~¥5,000 (≈ ₹2,900). *What it's for:* rainy-day thrills and VR without leaving the city.
54. **Small Worlds Miniature Museum** — *Ariake (Tokyo Bay).* Asia's largest miniature museum — Evangelion, a working airport, a space centre in 1/80 scale. Adult ~¥2,700 (≈ ₹1,565). *What it's for:* jaw-dropping model craftsmanship, great for photos.
55. **Capsule-hotel night** — *e.g. 9h nine hours (Shinjuku/Shibuya).* Sleep in a futuristic pod; from ~¥3,500–¥6,000 (≈ ₹2,030–₹3,480). *What it's for:* ticking off the pod-hotel bucket-list experience.

## 7. Views & photo spots

56. **Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building** — *Nishi-Shinjuku.* **FREE** 45F observatory (202m); North & South decks, a Yayoi-Kusama-designed piano, and a nightly building **projection-mapping show** (sunset–~21:45). ~09:30–22:00. *What it's for:* the best free panorama (Skytree, Fuji on clear days).
57. **Bunkyo Civic Center** — *Korakuen / Bunkyo.* **FREE** 25F observatory with a superb west-facing Mt-Fuji-at-sunset angle. ~09:00–20:30. *What it's for:* an under-the-radar free Fuji-and-skyline shot.
58. **Roppongi Hills — Tokyo City View / Mori Tower** — *Roppongi.* Indoor 52F deck (sometimes open-air Sky Deck) with the best straight-on **Tokyo Tower** shot. ~¥2,000+ (≈ ₹1,160+), often bundled with Mori Art Museum. *What it's for:* the postcard Tokyo-Tower-glowing-at-night photo.
59. **Shibuya crossing from above** — *Shibuya.* Free angles from the Shibuya Tsutaya **Starbucks** window and **Mag's Park** rooftop (small fee); or pay for Shibuya Sky. *What it's for:* the crowd-swarm-from-above shot.
60. **Chureito Pagoda (Fuji + pagoda)** — *Fujiyoshida, ~2hr from Tokyo (NOT in the city — flag as a day trip).* Free; climb ~400 steps to the classic five-storey-pagoda-with-Mt-Fuji composition, unreal in autumn foliage. *What it's for:* the single most iconic "Japan" photo — worth the trip on a clear day.

## 8. Shopping

61. **Akihabara** — *Chiyoda.* Anime/manga (Animate, Mandarake), retro games, electronics (Yodobashi), figures, doujinshi. *What it's for:* otaku and gadget hunting.
62. **Harajuku (Takeshita-dori + Omotesando)** — teen street fashion, kawaii accessories, crepes vs luxury flagships next door. *What it's for:* Japanese youth fashion, high to low.
63. **Ginza** — global luxury flagships (Chanel, Hermès), the 12-floor **Ginza Six**, Uniqlo/Muji mega-stores, Itoya stationery. *What it's for:* high-end and design shopping.
64. **Nakamise-dori** — *Asakusa.* Historic stall street to Senso-ji: fans, yukata, senbei, sweets, kitsch souvenirs. *What it's for:* traditional gifts and snacks.
65. **Don Quijote ("Donki")** — *Shinjuku (24h), citywide.* Chaotic multi-floor discount emporium: snacks, cosmetics, gadgets, costumes, souvenirs. *What it's for:* late-night one-stop souvenir/snack haul.
66. **Kappabashi "Kitchen Town"** — *near Asakusa/Ueno.* ~1km of chef supplies: Japanese knives, ceramics and the famous plastic **food samples**. *What it's for:* knives and quirky kitchen souvenirs.
67. **Nakano Broadway** — *Nakano.* Retro/collector's mecca (Mandarake HQ): vintage anime cels, figures, watches, toys — often cheaper and rarer than Akihabara. *What it's for:* serious collectible treasure-hunting.
68. **Ameyoko Market** — *Ueno/Okachimachi.* Open-air market under the train tracks: dried seafood, sweets, sneakers, cosmetics, cheap eats. *What it's for:* bargain-hunting and street food.
69. **Flea markets** — *Oedo Antique Market (Tokyo Int'l Forum, ~1st/3rd Sun) & shrine/temple markets.* Antiques, kimono, ceramics. *What it's for:* one-of-a-kind vintage finds (check dates before going).

## 9. Parks, nature & water within the city

70. **Yoyogi Park** — *Harajuku.* Big open lawns, buskers, rockabilly dancers on Sundays; free. *What it's for:* picnic-and-people-watch energy next to Meiji Jingu.
71. **Shinjuku Gyoen** — *Shinjuku.* Manicured Japanese/English/French gardens, glasshouse; **¥500** (≈ ₹290), ~09:00–16:30, closed Mon. *What it's for:* the city's best all-round garden and blossom/foliage spot.
72. **Rikugien** — *Komagome.* Classic Edo strolling garden with a teahouse; **¥300** (≈ ₹175). Special evening **autumn maple illuminations** (late Nov). *What it's for:* the most refined maple-viewing in Tokyo.
73. **Sumida River cruise** — *Asakusa ⇄ Hinode ⇄ Odaiba.* Futuristic **Hotaluna/Himiko** boats; Asakusa→Odaiba ~**¥1,560** (≈ ₹905). *What it's for:* seeing Skytree, bridges and the bay from the water.
74. **Odaiba Seaside Park & Rainbow Bridge** — *Odaiba.* Bayfront promenade, beach, sunset Rainbow-Bridge views, the Gundam statue at DiverCity. **Free**. *What it's for:* breezy bayside evening stroll.
75. **Ueno Park** — *Ueno.* Sprawling free park with museums, a pond, shrines and the zoo. *What it's for:* culture-plus-green in one hit.

## 10. Theme parks & big attractions

76. **Tokyo DisneySea** — *Maihama, Chiba (~25 min from Tokyo Stn).* The nautical-themed park unique to Tokyo; the new **Fantasy Springs** (Frozen/Tangled/Peter Pan) opened 2024. 1-Day Passport is **date-priced ~¥7,900–¥10,900** (≈ ₹4,580–₹6,320); Fantasy Springs needs paid Premier Access or a standby pass. *What it's for:* the world's most beautiful, adults-love-it Disney park — book ahead.
77. **Tokyo Disneyland** — *Maihama.* The classic castle park, same date-priced tickets. *What it's for:* the full Disney-magic day.
78. **Sanrio Puroland** — *Tama, west Tokyo.* Indoor Hello Kitty/My Melody/Kuromi theme park with parades and shows. Weekday **from ¥3,620** (≈ ₹2,100), pricier weekends. *What it's for:* full-on kawaii immersion, rain-proof.
79. **Warner Bros. Studio Tour Tokyo — The Making of Harry Potter** — *Nerima (Toshimaen site).* Walk the real Great Hall, Diagon Alley, Forbidden Forest sets. Adult ~¥6,300 (≈ ₹3,655; a price update applies from July 2026). **Timed entry, book weeks ahead.** *What it's for:* the world's largest indoor Harry Potter attraction.
80. **Yomiuri Giants baseball @ Tokyo Dome** — *Bunkyo.* Electric, chanting, beer-girl-fuelled NPB baseball; regular tickets ~¥1,800–¥6,000 (≈ ₹1,045–₹3,480). The **Central League Climax Series (playoffs) is mid-October** (2025 ran Oct 11–17) — huge atmosphere but **instant sell-outs, book the moment they release.** *What it's for:* Japanese baseball culture at its loudest.

## 11. October / autumn-specific in Tokyo

81. **Autumn foliage (koyo) timing** — Tokyo's maples/ginkgo peak **late Nov–early Dec**, so October is early; for October colour, do a day trip to higher/cooler spots (Nikko, Mt. Takao, Kawaguchiko). *What it's for:* set expectations — October is crisp and pleasant (highs ~22°C) but not yet peak-red in the city centre.
82. **Meiji Jingu Gaien Ginkgo Avenue** — *Aoyama.* Tokyo's most famous golden-ginkgo tunnel; colour usually **mid-to-late Nov**. Free. *What it's for:* the classic autumn-in-Tokyo walk.
83. **Tokyo Grand Tea Ceremony** — *Edo-Tokyo Open Air Architectural Museum (Oct 19–20) & Hama-rikyu (Oct 26–27), 2025 dates.* Large public tea event across historic settings. *What it's for:* accessible traditional tea in autumn.
84. **Tokyo Ramen Festa** — *Komazawa Olympic Park, ~Oct 23–Nov 3.* Dozens of regional ramen styles in one field. *What it's for:* a ramen-lover's autumn festival.
85. **Hama-rikyu moonlight opening** — *Chuo.* One rare week of extended evening hours with lantern-lit paths and Noh/koto/shamisen performances. *What it's for:* atmospheric night-garden culture.
86. **Yomiuriland "Jewellumination"** — *Inagi, west Tokyo.* One of the region's biggest illumination shows, opening ~**Oct 23** (runs into spring). *What it's for:* early-season winter-lights spectacle.
87. **Autumn seasonal foods** — chestnut (kuri) and sweet-potato (satsumaimo) sweets, matsutake mushrooms, momiji-shaped wagashi, and limited-edition pumpkin/chestnut lattes everywhere. *What it's for:* eating the season — hunt konbini and depachika limited editions.

## 12. Hidden gems / offbeat

88. **Nezu Shrine** — *Bunkyo.* A gorgeous vermilion **torii-gate tunnel** (a mini Fushimi-Inari) and azalea garden, minus the Kyoto crowds. Free. *What it's for:* the torii shot without the queue.
89. **Yanaka Ginza & Yanaka Cemetery** — *Yanaka.* Retro shopping street, sunset "Yuyake Dandan" steps, cat-spotting and quiet temple lanes. Free. *What it's for:* time-capsule old Tokyo.
90. **Jimbocho book town** — *Chiyoda.* ~150 second-hand bookshops, vintage prints and old-school coffee houses. Free to wander. *What it's for:* bibliophile browsing and cheap ukiyo-e/prints.
91. **Kagurazaka** — *Shinjuku.* Cobbled former geisha district: stone alleys, hidden French bistros and old ryotei. *What it's for:* a chic, romantic, low-tourist evening stroll.
92. **Todoroki Valley (Todoroki Keikoku)** — *Setagaya.* A leafy ravine walk along a stream, minutes from a train line — feels like countryside inside Tokyo. Free. *What it's for:* an unexpected nature escape (nice in autumn).
93. **Golden Gai by day** — *Shinjuku.* The tiny-bar maze is eerily photogenic and calm before the evening crowds. Free to walk. *What it's for:* atmospheric photos without the cover charges.
94. **Nonbei Yokocho ("Drunkard's Alley")** — *Shibuya.* A sliver of tiny post-war bars steps from the scramble; far more local than Golden Gai. *What it's for:* an intimate drink hidden in plain sight.

A field guide for young adults who want *experiences* — hikes, onsen, coasters, canyoning,
retro-Edo streets, island caves — but who also don't want to miss the postcard icons.
Every entry has: how to get there from Tokyo, the vibe, the best things to do, and who it's for.

**Currency:** prices are in Japanese yen (¥). Approx. INR shown at **¥100 ≈ ₹58** (so ₹ ≈ ¥ × 0.58).
Rates and hours drift — reconfirm the week you travel. Fares below are one-way from central
Tokyo unless noted.

## How to use this list (quick picks)

**Best for OCTOBER (autumn foliage / onsen season):**
- **Nikko** — the region's foliage cascades down by altitude through October; Okunikko plateau early Oct, Lake Chuzenji mid-Oct, **Irohazaka switchbacks late Oct** (peak). The signature October trip.
- **Hakone** — foliage + onsen; crisp air, Fuji visibility improves in autumn.
- **Mt. Takao** — Tokyo's most accessible koyo; maples on Trail 1 peak *late* November, but pleasant hiking weather all October.
- **Kawagoe** — the **Kawagoe Festival falls on Oct 17–18, 2026** (3rd Sat/Sun); a UNESCO float festival, one of the region's biggest events.
- **Mt. Fuji / Kawaguchiko** — snow can cap Fuji from early October; clear autumn skies = best Fuji odds. **Chichibu Night Festival** is Dec (not Oct).
- **Chichibu** shibazakura is a *spring* (Apr–May) event — not an October pick.

**Needs ADVANCE BOOKING:**
- **Fuji-Q Highland** — buy timed date-tickets online (cheaper + skips the gate queue); popular coasters have long same-day lines.
- **Edo Wonderland (Nikko)** — book online for a discount; closed most Wednesdays.
- **Okutama rafting / canyoning** — reserve a slot (Apr–Nov season only); guides sell out on weekends.
- **Kusatsu / Ikaho onsen** — really an overnight; if staying, book the ryokan early (esp. autumn weekends).
- **Sawara / Little Edo Sawara boat tour** — small boats; reserve on busy days.
- **Regional passes** (Hakone Free Pass, Nikko Pass, Odakyu Enoshima-Kamakura Free Pass) — buy at the origin station before you set off.

## Nikko (~2 hr) — shrines, waterfalls, and the October foliage crown

**Getting there:** The scenic way is the **Tobu Limited Express Spacia / Spacia X from Asakusa** to
Tobu-Nikko, ~1h50–2h. JR routes also exist via Utsunomiya. For the day, grab the **Nikko World
Heritage Area Pass (~¥3,000 / ₹1,740, 2-day** — round-trip train + local shrine-area buses) or the
**Nikko All Area Pass (~¥8,000 / ₹4,640, 4-day)** which adds the Chuzenji/Kegon buses. From the
Shinkyo Bridge area, buses climb ~50 min to Kegon Falls and Lake Chuzenji.

**Vibe:** Grand and cinematic — mountain forest, mist, gilded shrines, and thundering water.
Half culture pilgrimage, half nature escape. In late October it's *the* foliage destination.

**Do:**
1. **Toshogu Shrine** — the lavishly carved UNESCO mausoleum of shogun Tokugawa Ieyasu (see-no/hear-no/speak-no-evil monkeys, the sleeping cat).
2. **Rinnoji Temple & Futarasan-jinja** — the other two UNESCO components; combine with Toshogu on a shrine-area pass.
3. **Kegon Falls** — a 97 m plunge, one of Japan's three great waterfalls; elevator to the base viewing deck.
4. **Lake Chuzenji** — a serene crater lake at ~1,270 m; boat rides and lakeside walks.
5. **Irohazaka Winding Road** — 48 hairpin switchbacks; the drive/bus up is a foliage spectacle in **late October**.
6. **Edo Wonderland Nikko Edomura** — a live Edo-town theme park: ninja & samurai shows, period costume rental, throwing stars. **All-day ¥5,800 / ₹3,364; afternoon ¥5,000 / ₹2,900**; ~9:00–17:00, closed most Wed. (**Book online for a discount.**)
7. **Kanmangafuchi Abyss** — a quiet riverside gorge lined with rows of Jizo statues; great walk.

**Best for:** Culture-plus-nature travelers, and **the single strongest October foliage pick** —
elevation staggers the color from early Oct (Okunikko) to late Oct (Irohazaka). Long day; start early.

---

## Hakone (~1.5 hr) — the onsen–art–Fuji loop with a pirate ship

**Getting there:** **Odakyu Romancecar from Shinjuku** to Hakone-Yumoto (~85 min). Buy the
**Hakone Free Pass** — unlimited mountain train, cablecar, ropeway, buses, and the Lake Ashi
sightseeing boat, plus attraction discounts. **From Shinjuku: 2-day ¥7,100 / ₹4,118, 3-day
¥7,500 / ₹4,350; Hakone-area-only 2-day ¥6,000 / ₹3,480** (prices as of Oct 2025). The classic
"Hakone loop" chains train → cablecar → ropeway → boat → bus back into one continuous circuit.

**Vibe:** A refined mountain resort — steam vents, lakeside torii, sculpture in the open air, and
Fuji peeking over the ridgeline. Relaxed but full of things to string together.

**Do:**
1. **Owakudani** — a live volcanic valley of sulfur vents reached by ropeway; eat a **kuro-tamago** (black egg boiled in the springs, said to add 7 years of life). Best Fuji views on clear days.
2. **Lake Ashi pirate-ship cruise** — a mock European galleon crosses the lake (~25–40 min); covered by the Free Pass.
3. **Hakone Open-Air Museum** — sculpture park with Picasso pavilion and a stained-glass tower; **¥2,000 door / ¥1,800 online (₹1,160 / ₹1,044)**, 9:00–17:00.
4. **Hakone Shrine & the "Torii of Peace"** — the vermilion gate standing in Lake Ashi; iconic photo, expect a queue.
5. **Old Tokaido cedar avenue** — walk the Edo-era stone highway under ~400 towering, 400-year-old cedars from Moto-Hakone; short and atmospheric.
6. **Onsen soak** — end at a Hakone-Yumoto bathhouse or ryokan day-bath.
7. **Hakone Checkpoint (Sekisho)** — reconstructed Edo-period border-control post on the lake.

**Best for:** Onsen + art + scenery in one smooth loop; couples and groups who want variety without
much walking. **October** brings foliage and better Fuji odds. Booking optional (buy the pass at
Shinjuku).

---

## Kamakura + Enoshima (~1 hr) — Great Buddha, surf coast, and island caves

**Getting there:** **JR Yokosuka Line** from Tokyo/Shinagawa to Kamakura (~1 hr), then hop the
retro **Enoden** tram down the coast. Consider the **Odakyu Enoshima–Kamakura Free Pass** for the
Enoden + area. Enoden Kamakura→Enoshima is ~25 min (~¥220 / ₹128).

**Vibe:** Japan's laid-back seaside samurai capital — temples in wooded hills on one side, a surf
beach and a sacred island on the other. The most "young-adult experiences" combo near Tokyo:
history in the morning, beach and caves by afternoon.

**Do:**
1. **Great Buddha (Kotoku-in)** — the 13.35 m bronze Daibutsu (1252); **¥300 / ₹174** entry, **+¥50 / ₹29 to step inside** the hollow statue.
2. **Hase-dera** — hillside temple with a giant wooden Kannon, sea-view terrace, and famous hydrangeas; **¥400 / ₹232**.
3. **Enoden coastal ride** — the tram nearly brushes the houses; jump off near **Kamakura-Koko-Mae** for the anime-famous ocean-and-crossing view.
4. **Surf / beach time** at Yuigahama or Shichirigahama — board rental, cafes, sunset over Sagami Bay.
5. **Enoshima island** — cross the bridge and climb the shrine-lined lanes.
6. **Enoshima Sea Candle** observation tower — **~¥800 / ₹464** for panoramic bay-and-Fuji views.
7. **Iwaya sea caves** — lantern-lit caves at the island's far tip; **~¥500 / ₹290**.
8. **Kamakura hiking trails** — the ~3 km Daibutsu/Kuzuharagaoka trail links temples through the hills, passing the **Zeniarai Benzaiten** cave money-washing shrine.

**Best for:** The do-everything day — history buffs, beach/surf types, and photographers all win.
No advance booking needed; go early on summer/autumn weekends to beat crowds.

---

## Mt. Fuji / Fuji Five Lakes / Kawaguchiko (~2 hr) — icons plus a world-class coaster park

**Getting there:** Direct **"Fuji Excursion" limited express from Shinjuku** (~2 hr, a few daily),
or Chuo Line to Otsuki → Fujikyuko Line to Kawaguchiko. Cheapest is the **direct highway bus from
Shinjuku (~2 hr, ¥2,200 / ₹1,276; ~¥2,000 / ₹1,160 online)**, stopping at Fujisan Station and Fuji-Q
before Kawaguchiko. Fuji-Q sits at its own station, 2 min / ¥180 (₹104) from Kawaguchiko.

**Vibe:** Big-sky Fuji country — mirror-lake reflections, a red pagoda framing the peak, spring-fed
ponds, and the country's most infamous roller coasters. Snow can cap Fuji from **early October**,
and autumn air gives the clearest views of the year.

**Do:**
1. **Chureito Pagoda** — the five-story red pagoda above Arakurayama with Fuji behind; ~400 steps up, the definitive Fuji photo (best clear mornings).
2. **Mt. Fuji Panoramic Ropeway (a.k.a. Mt. Tenjo / "Kachi Kachi")** — 3-min lift to a 1,075 m deck over Lake Kawaguchi; **~¥1,000 / ₹580** round trip; "Heavenly Bell," rabbit-shrine kitsch, and an optional **Super View Swing (¥500–¥1,000 / ₹290–580)**.
3. **Fuji-Q Highland** — free entry, pay-per-ride, or **1-day pass ¥6,000–7,800 / ₹3,480–4,524** (after-2pm ¥4,100–5,900 / ₹2,378–3,422). Record-breaking coasters: **Fujiyama, Takabisha** (121° drop, steepest in the world), **Eejanaika** (most inversions), and the newer **Zokkon** motorbike coaster. ~9:00–17:00. **Book timed tickets online.**
4. **Oshino Hakkai** — eight crystal-clear spring ponds fed by Fuji snowmelt filtered through lava; a designated natural monument, thatched-roof village setting.
5. **Oishi Park (Lake Kawaguchi north shore)** — flower fields foreground + Fuji; classic lakeside stroll.
6. **Lake Kawaguchi reflections** — "Sakasa-Fuji" (upside-down Fuji) on still mornings.

**Best for:** Splitting the day between Fuji icons and adrenaline (coasters). Thrill-seekers +
photographers. **October** for snow-capped clarity; **Fuji-Q needs advance booking.**

---

## Yokohama (~25–30 min) — the easy futuristic-harbor half-day

**Getting there:** Several lines: **JR Tokaido Line** from Tokyo Station (~25 min), JR Yokosuka
from Shinagawa, or **Tokyu Toyoko Line from Shibuya** (~30 min) which continues onto the Minatomirai
Line straight to the waterfront (no transfer).

**Vibe:** Sleek port-city cool — glassy skyscrapers and a Ferris wheel over the bay by day, neon
harbor by night, and the biggest Chinatown in Japan for eating your way around. The lowest-effort
day trip on this list; great for an evening too.

**Do:**
1. **Yokohama Chinatown** — Japan's largest, 400+ restaurants and street-food stalls; free to wander, graze on nikuman, tapioca, xiao long bao.
2. **Minato Mirai 21** — futuristic waterfront of malls, museums, and promenades; free to roam.
3. **Cup Noodles Museum** — design-your-own custom cup noodle; 10-min walk from Minatomirai or Sakuragicho.
4. **Cosmo World & Cosmo Clock 21** — bayside amusement park with the giant Ferris wheel (pay-per-ride).
5. **Sankeien Garden** — a 175,000 m² traditional landscape garden with relocated historic buildings and a pagoda; reached by bus, a calm counterpoint to the neon.
6. **Red Brick Warehouse (Aka Renga)** — restored brick harbor buildings, now shops, cafes, and seasonal events.

**Best for:** A relaxed food-and-waterfront half-day, or a night out; couples and groups who want
city energy without a long haul. No booking needed.

---

## Kawagoe (~30–45 min) — "Little Edo" warehouse streets and candy alley

**Getting there:** **~30 min from Ikebukuro** by Tobu Tojo Line (to Kawagoe/Kawagoe-shi) or Seibu
Shinjuku Line (to Hon-Kawagoe). One of the closest, cheapest day trips going.

**Vibe:** A time-capsule of old Edo that Tokyo itself lost to fires and war — 200+ fireproof
*kurazukuri* clay warehouses lining a merchant street, plus a nostalgic sweets lane. Photogenic,
snacky, and easy; rent a kimono to lean into it.

**Do:**
1. **Kurazukuri Street** — the main warehouse-architecture strip of shops and restaurants.
2. **Toki-no-Kane (Bell of Time)** — the wooden bell tower that's Kawagoe's symbol.
3. **Kashiya Yokocho (Candy Alley)** — a lane of traditional confectioners; sample dagashi, giant senbei, sweet-potato treats (Kawagoe's specialty).
4. **Kita-in Temple** — home to 540 unique stone rakan (disciple) statues, no two faces alike.
5. **Hikawa Shrine** — famous for summer wind-chime tunnels and a heart-shaped ema wall; the source of the big festival.
6. **Kimono rental + street food** — the low-key headline "experience" here.

**Best for:** Retro-Japan atmosphere and snacking on a short leash from Tokyo. **October highlight:
the Kawagoe Festival, Oct 17–18, 2026** — a 360+ year-old, UNESCO-listed float festival with
after-dark "Hikkawase" float face-offs. Expect big crowds those two days.

---

## Mt. Takao (~1 hr) — Tokyo's most accessible sacred-mountain hike

**Getting there:** **Keio Line from Shinjuku** direct to Takaosanguchi (~50 min, ~¥400 / ₹232) —
about the easiest mountain access anywhere near Tokyo.

**Vibe:** A 599 m holy mountain that's a genuine hike but doable in sneakers, with a mountaintop
temple, monkey park, forest trails, and an onsen at the trailhead. Beginner-friendly nature with a
real summit payoff (Fuji on clear days).

**Do:**
1. **Ride the cablecar / chairlift** — the funicular climbs a 31° max grade (Japan's steepest) in ~6 min; skip the first stretch or hike it (**~¥490 one-way / ~¥950 round-trip, ₹284 / ₹551, approx**).
2. **Trail 1 to the summit** — paved main route past shops and viewpoints; other numbered trails (e.g. Trail 6 along a stream) are wilder.
3. **Yakuo-in Temple** — a mountainside temple with tengu (goblin) imagery en route to the top.
4. **Summit + Fuji view** — clear-day Mt. Fuji panorama from the 599 m top.
5. **Monkey Park & wildflower garden** near the cablecar upper station.
6. **Keio Takaosan Onsen Gokurakuyu** — a large hot-spring complex *right next to the station* for a post-hike soak.

**Best for:** First-time hikers, foliage day-trippers, and anyone wanting a nature+onsen combo without
committing to a big trek. **October** is lovely for hiking; maple **koyo peaks late November** (very
crowded then). No booking; go on a weekday to dodge cablecar queues.

---

## Nokogiriyama (~2 hr, Chiba) — the cliff "hell peek" and Japan's biggest stone Buddha

**Getting there:** **JR Uchibo Line** to Hama-Kanaya (Sazanami limited express ~1.5 hr from Tokyo
Station), then 10 min on foot to the ropeway. A fun alternative: the **Tokyo Bay ferry** from
Kurihama (Kanagawa side) to Kanaya. **Ropeway ¥1,200 / ₹696 round trip (¥650 / ₹377 one-way).**

**Vibe:** An offbeat, slightly wild half-day — a 329 m quarried peak on the Boso Peninsula with sheer
cut cliffs, a nerve-testing lookout, forest steps, and a colossal Buddha. Less polished than the big
names, which is the appeal.

**Do:**
1. **Jigoku Nozoki ("Hell Peek")** — stand on a rock ledge jutting off a sheer cliff over the drop; the signature photo.
2. **Nihon-ji Daibutsu** — a **31 m seated stone Buddha**, the largest of its kind in Japan (taller than Kamakura's or Nara's).
3. **Hyaku-shaku Kannon** — a large relief carved into a quarry wall.
4. **Ropeway or stone-step hike** — ride up for ease, or climb from the base for the earned view; ~30-min stone-step descent links the summit to the Daibutsu plaza.
5. **Summit panorama** — Boso Peninsula, Tokyo Bay, and on clear days Mt. Fuji and Skytree.
6. **Ferry crossing** — build the Tokyo Bay ferry into the trip as its own mini-experience.

**Best for:** Adventurous day-trippers wanting a hike + jaw-drop viewpoint + something most tourists
skip. Wear real shoes; combine with the ferry for maximum fun.

---

## Sawara (~1h50, Chiba) — the canal town where you cruise past Edo

**Getting there:** **JR Sobu/Narita Line rapid to Narita, change to the Narita Line to Sawara**
(~1h50 from Tokyo Station); the boat dock is ~13 min walk from Sawara Station. Pairs well with a
Narita/airport-area day.

**Vibe:** A quieter "Little Edo" than Kawagoe — willow-lined canals, black-tiled merchant houses,
and a slow river cruise. Sleepy, photogenic, and refreshingly un-touristy.

**Do:**
1. **Little Edo Sawara boat tour** — a covered wooden boat glides the Ono River through the historic center; the town's headline experience. (**Reserve on busy days.**)
2. **Ono River old townscape** — stroll the preserved Edo-Meiji merchant streets along the water.
3. **Ino Tadataka former residence & museum** — home of the surveyor who first accurately mapped Japan.
4. **Suigo Sawara Ayame Park** — 1.5 million irises (Asia's largest collection), peaking with a June festival.

**Best for:** A calm, offbeat, photo-friendly day; travelers who've "done" Kawagoe and want the
slower version. Best paired with good weather.

---

## Chichibu (~80 min, Saitama) — mountain valley and the spring flower carpet

**Getting there:** **Seibu Ikebukuro Line** from Ikebukuro to Seibu-Chichibu (~80 min on the
**Limited Express "Laview"/Red Arrow**); ~20-min walk to Hitsujiyama Park.

**Vibe:** A relaxed mountain-valley town ringed by peaks — flowers, shrines, river gorges, and
festivals. Best known for a spring flower spectacle, but a pleasant nature base year-round.

**Do:**
1. **Hitsujiyama Park shibazakura** — the Kanto region's largest moss-phlox field (~17,600 m²) blooms in patterned pink carpets **mid-April to early May** (~¥300 / ₹174 in season, 8:00–17:00).
2. **Chichibu Shrine** — the old town's centerpiece, tied to the famous December festival.
3. **Nagatoro river gorge & boat rides** — nearby valley with rock formations and traditional wooden riverboats.
4. **Chichibu Night Festival (Dec 2–3)** — one of Japan's great float festivals, with illuminated floats and winter fireworks (note: **December, not October**).

**Best for:** Spring flower-hunters and nature day-trippers. Not an October pick (its headline events
are April–May and December).

---

## Kusatsu / Ikaho Onsen (~2.5–4 hr — over 2 hr; overnight recommended)

**Getting there (over the ~2 hr guideline):**
- **Kusatsu:** Limited Express to **Naganohara-Kusatsuguchi**, then JR bus ~30 min (¥780 / ₹452) to the Kusatsu bus terminal (6-min walk to the Yubatake). Or a **direct highway bus from Tokyo/Shinjuku, ~4 hr**.
- **Ikaho:** ~1h15 by train to the nearest station, then ~20-min bus to the onsen town (rail portion ~¥3,800 / ₹2,204). Local buses link the Ikaho/Kusatsu areas.

**Vibe:** Japan's onsen heartland (Gunma). Kusatsu is one of the country's most famous, highest-volume
hot-spring resorts, built around a steaming central water field; Ikaho is the atmospheric "stone-step"
onsen town. Doable as a long day, but far better as an **overnight ryokan trip.**

**Do:**
1. **Kusatsu Yubatake ("hot water field")** — the steaming, sulfur-scented centerpiece that gushes mineral water through wooden channels; incredible lit up at night.
2. **Yumomi performance** — traditional cooling of the scalding water by paddle, with song, at Netsunoyu.
3. **Sainokawara open-air rotenburo** — a large outdoor bath in a riverside park.
4. **Ikaho stone stairway (Ishidan-gai)** — 365+ steps lined with ryokan, game halls, and shops; retro onsen-town charm.
5. **Public bathhouse hopping** — the real experience in both towns.

**Best for:** Onsen devotees, especially in **October's crisp weather** (steam + autumn = peak
onsen mood). **Over the 2-hr guideline** and better overnight — **book the ryokan early** for autumn
weekends.

## Adventure & nature (rafting, canyoning, gorges) — Okutama / Mitake (~1.5 hr)

**Getting there:** **JR Chuo/Ome Line** to Mitake/Okutama (~90 min from Shinjuku); rafting and
canyoning bases are ~5-min walk from JR Mitake Station, right on the Tama River.

**Vibe:** Tokyo's own wild frontier — clear mountain river, forested gorge, and the closest real
whitewater/canyoning to the capital. The most genuinely adrenal "experience" day on this list.

**Do:**
1. **Tama River rafting** — rapids + calm-water play through the Mitake Gorge; beginner- and family-friendly options (from ~elementary age). **Season roughly April–November.**
2. **Canyoning** — slide natural rock chutes, jump off ledges into pools, and rope down waterfalls in the gorge.
3. **Rafting + canyoning combo** courses for a full adrenaline day (guided, lunch often included).
4. **Mitake Gorge riverside walk** — one of Japan's "100 best waters"; scenic even without getting wet.
5. **Mt. Mitake & Musashi-Mitake Shrine** — cablecar up to a mountaintop shrine village and easy ridge hikes; pairs with a morning on the river.
6. **Lake Okutama** — reservoir scenery further up the valley for a quieter nature add-on.

**Best for:** Thrill-seekers and active groups who want Tokyo-close nature with a rush. **Reserve
your slot in advance** (Apr–Nov only; weekends sell out). October = cooler water but gorgeous gorge
foliage building up.

## Honorable mentions (also within/near range)

- **Boso Peninsula beaches (Chiba)** — beyond Nokogiriyama, the Boso coast has surf beaches and seaside towns for a laid-back summer/early-autumn day.
- **Enoshima alone** — if short on time, Enoshima (shrine, Sea Candle, Iwaya caves) works as a standalone coastal half-day via the Odakyu line.
- **Narita** — not just the airport: Naritasan Shinshoji Temple and its old approach street make an easy ~1 hr stop, and it chains neatly with Sawara.

# PART 3 — KYOTO (in the city)

For young adults who want *real* experiences, without skipping the classic must-sees.
Prices are 2025–2026 published rates in **¥ (approx ₹ at ¥100 ≈ ₹58)**; always re-check
official sites before you go — temple fees creep up and illuminations change yearly.

> **The one rule that changes everything:** Kyoto's famous sites are *mobbed* from
> ~9am to 4pm (worst 11am–3pm). Front-load the icons **before 8–9am** or do them at
> **dusk/night**; spend the crowded midday hours indoors (museums, cafés, kaiseki lunch,
> markets, a tea ceremony). Weekends + Japanese public holidays are the worst.

### Book these in ADVANCE (they sell out / need reservation)

# PART 4 — OSAKA (in the city)

> **Osaka = Japan's party-and-food capital.** The local word is **kuidaore** ("eat yourself
> bankrupt / eat till you drop"). This is the loud, greasy, neon, comedy-loving, stay-out-till-
> first-train antidote to buttoned-up Kyoto. For a high-energy young-adult group, it's the single
> best-value fun city in Japan.
>
> **Currency:** ¥100 ≈ ₹58 (¥1 ≈ ₹0.58). ¥ prices are current 2025–2026; ₹ is approximate.
> **Verdict up front (see bottom):** Osaka is worth a solid **1.5–2 full days** for an energetic
> group — do NOT treat it as a Kyoto day-trip base only. It earns its own nights.

## Verdict: How many days? Osaka vs Kyoto day-trip

- **Give Osaka 1.5–2 full days minimum** for an energetic food-and-party group — do NOT reduce it
  to just a Kyoto commuter base. Its nightlife, street food, USJ, and comedy are things Kyoto simply
  doesn't have. A common, well-balanced Kansai split is **~2 days Osaka + ~4 days Kyoto**.
- **Kyoto is an easy day-trip** — only ~12–15 min by shinkansen / ~40 min by regular rail from
  Osaka — but Kyoto genuinely rewards 2 days (Fushimi Inari, Kinkaku-ji, Arashiyama). If your group
  is high-energy/food-and-fun focused, weight nights toward **Osaka**, days toward Kyoto.
- **Ideal energetic-group Osaka 2-day skeleton:** Day 1 = **USJ + Super Nintendo World**, then
  Dotonbori dinner + Ura-Namba/Ame-mura nightlife. Day 2 = Osaka Castle → Kuromon Market lunch →
  Shinsekai/Spa World → Umeda Sky sunset → Namba Grand Kagetsu comedy → karaoke till first train.

## Bonus: Comedy capital (don't skip)

- **Namba Grand Kagetsu (NGK)** — Namba. Japan's **largest comedy theater** (858 seats), home of
  Yoshimoto Kogyo. **Manzai** (duo stand-up boke/tsukkomi), konto sketches & slapstick **Shinkigeki**
  365 days/yr. Osaka IS Japan's comedy capital; some shows use simple English / visual humor. Tickets
  ~¥2,000–5,000 / ₹1,160–2,900. The lighter **Yoshimoto Dotonbori Theater** is a shorter option.

## FLAG: Advance-booking essentials

- **★ Super Nintendo World needs TIMED ENTRY.** It is not automatically included with a park ticket

# PART 5 — KANSAI DAY TRIPS (1–2 hours from Kyoto/Osaka)

*For young adults who want experiences, not just checklists. Real names, real prices, honest travel times.*
*Currency: ¥100 ≈ ₹58. Prices are 2025–2026 and approximate — always reconfirm on the day.*
*"Closest base" = which of Kyoto / Osaka is the easier launch point.*

---

## How to read this

Everything from **Nara** down to **Minoo Falls** is a genuine ≤~1.5h day trip — go, do it, be back for dinner.
Everything under **"Honest 2h+ calls"** (Amanohashidate, Kinosaki, Nachi/Kumano) is *longer* than 2 hours each way; I've kept them because they're worth it, but I've flagged the real cost in hours so you can decide.

**October note up front:** Kansai's autumn foliage (*koyo*) generally peaks **mid-November to early December**, so early-mid October is usually still green — you get comfortable ~20–25°C hiking weather and light crowds rather than red maples. Onsen and gardens are excellent in October regardless. Where October matters, each section says so.

**Two passes worth knowing:** the **Kintetsu Rail Pass** (Kyoto–Nara–Osaka–Ise) and the **Kansai region JR passes** cover most of these. For onsen towns like Kinosaki, check the **JR Limited Express** direct services.

## 1. Nara — the bowing deer + a Buddha the size of a house
**Closest base:** Kyoto *or* Osaka (equally easy) · **Travel:** ~35–45 min

**Getting there:** From Kyoto, Kintetsu Limited Express to **Kintetsu-Nara Station** (~35–45 min) or JR Nara Line to JR Nara. From Osaka (Namba), Kintetsu Nara Line (~40 min). Kintetsu-Nara Station is closer to the park than JR Nara.

**Vibe:** Japan's first permanent capital — ancient, green, and gloriously weird thanks to 1,200+ free-roaming sika deer that will *bow* to you for a cracker. Big-hitter temples but a slow, park-y pace. Very walkable.

**Top things to do**
- **Nara Park** — buy *shika senbei* (deer crackers, ~¥200 / ₹116) and feed the bowing deer. They're wild; hold crackers high and they'll bow.
- **Todai-ji** — the Great Buddha Hall, one of the world's largest wooden buildings, houses a 15m bronze Buddha. Don't miss the Nandaimon gate guardians.
- **Kasuga Taisha** — vermilion shrine lined with 3,000+ stone and hanging bronze lanterns; atmospheric forest approach.
- **Kofuku-ji** — five-storied pagoda right in the park.
- **Isuien Garden** — a serene "borrowed scenery" strolling garden (admission ~¥1,200 / ₹696), the calm counterpoint to the deer chaos.
- **Naramachi** — old merchant quarter of lattice-front machiya houses, now cafés, craft shops and a good lunch stop (try kakinoha-zushi / persimmon-leaf sushi).

**Best for:** first-timers, animal-lovers, culture + photos in one easy hop.
**Plan:** 4–5h for the core loop; 7–8h to add gardens. Arrive before 10am.
**October / safety:** Great weather. **Heads up:** Sept–Nov is deer rutting season — bucks get aggressive (35 injuries recorded in Sept 2025 alone). Feed calmly, don't tease, keep bags closed.
**Booking:** None needed. Just go.

---

## 2. Uji — the matcha capital + the temple on the ¥10 coin
**Closest base:** Kyoto · **Travel:** ~20–30 min

**Getting there:** JR Nara Line from Kyoto Station to **JR Uji** (~30 min, ¥240 / ₹139), or Keihan Main Line from Gion (~45 min) to Keihan Uji.

**Vibe:** Riverside old-Japan town that has been *the* name in green tea for 800 years. Half-day, low-key, delicious. The whole main street smells of roasting tea.

**Top things to do**
- **Byodo-in** — the Phoenix Hall reflected in its pond (admission ¥700 / ₹406). It's the building on the back of the ¥10 coin — bring one and compare. Museum on-site.
- **Matcha experiences** — grind your own matcha on a stone mill (~¥1,000–1,500 / ₹580–870, ~1h) at shops like **Mitsuboshien Kanbayashi Sannyu**.
- **Tea shops & cafés** — **Nakamura Tokichi** and **Itohkyuemon** for matcha soba, matcha parfaits and jelly; **Tsuen** by Uji Bridge is one of Japan's oldest teahouses.
- **Ujigami Shrine** — UNESCO-listed, said to be Japan's oldest surviving shrine building (free).
- **Uji River & Uji Bridge** — riverside stroll, one of Japan's three oldest bridges.

**Best for:** matcha obsessives, foodies, an easy half-day paired with a lazy afternoon back in Kyoto.
**October:** Popular month — expect up to ~1.5h waits for the Phoenix Hall interior tour; go early. Weather ideal.
**Booking:** Optional — pre-book a tea-ceremony slot in peak season.

---

## 3. Arashiyama — *(a Kyoto sub-area, not really a day trip)*
**Closest base:** Kyoto (this is *in* Kyoto) · **Travel:** ~15–30 min from Kyoto Station

**Getting there:** JR Sagano Line to **Saga-Arashiyama** (~15 min, ¥240 / ₹139), or the Keifuku "Randen" tram from the Shijo area (~30 min) for the scenic approach.

**Vibe:** Kyoto's photogenic western edge — bamboo, a big river, temples and a monkey mountain. Include it as a half-day *within* your Kyoto time, not as a separate trip.

**Top things to do**
- **Bamboo Grove** — the iconic towering-bamboo path (free). Go 7–8am to beat the crush.
- **Tenryu-ji** — UNESCO Zen temple with a celebrated garden framing the mountains (¥500 / ₹290).
- **Iwatayama Monkey Park** — 20-min uphill hike to ~120 wild macaques and a city view (¥800 / ₹464 as of Oct 2025).
- **Togetsukyo Bridge** — the postcard bridge over the Katsura/Hozu river.
- **Hozugawa River boat ride** — traditional flat-bottomed boat down the gorge from Kameoka (~2h) through rapids; or rent a rowboat on the calm stretch by the bridge.

**Best for:** photos, an easy nature+temple morning inside Kyoto.
**October:** Foliage builds late in the month; the Hozugawa boat ride is spectacular once leaves turn (later Nov best).
**Booking:** None for the grove; boat ride can be reserved.

## 4. Kobe — beef, Chinatown, a foreign quarter, and a million-dollar night view
**Closest base:** Osaka · **Travel:** ~20–30 min from Osaka; ~50 min from Kyoto

**Getting there:** JR Special Rapid Osaka → **Kobe-Sannomiya** (~20 min); Shinkansen Shin-Osaka → Shin-Kobe (13 min); Hanshin from Osaka-Umeda (~30 min). Kyoto → Kobe ~50 min by JR.

**Vibe:** Stylish, cosmopolitan port city wedged between sea and mountains. Everything downtown (Sannomiya, Kitano, Chinatown, harbour) is a 10–20 min walk apart. Feels international and food-forward — a great "city day" with a knockout night finale.

**Top things to do**
- **Kobe beef** — the reason many come. A teppanyaki **lunch** typically runs ~¥5,000–8,000 (₹2,900–4,640); dinner ¥15,000+ (₹8,700+). Book ahead (e.g. Steakland, Mouriya, Tor Road Steak Aoyama).
- **Nankinmachi (Chinatown)** — lively street food: nikuman (pork buns), tapioca, ramen. Cheap and fun.
- **Kitano Ijinkan** — hillside district of preserved 19th-century Western merchant mansions from Kobe's port-opening era; walkable, café-dense.
- **Mt Rokko / Mt Maya night view** — the **Kikuseidai** platform on Mt Maya (ropeway + cable car from Sannomiya) delivers one of Japan's "three great night views" over Kobe and Osaka bay. Time your day to end here.
- **Nunobiki Herb Garden & Ropeway** — ropeway from beside Shin-Kobe (~¥1,500 / ₹870 round trip), terraced herb gardens + city panorama; open till 8:30pm on summer/peak weekends for evening views.
- **Harborland & Meriken Park** — waterfront with Kobe Port Tower, illuminations, and the Nunobiki Falls hike nearby.

**Best for:** foodies, city-lovers, couples/groups wanting a great night view. Easy to combine with Himeji or Arima.
**October:** Perfect. Clear autumn evenings = the best night views; herb garden still colourful.
**Booking:** Reserve Kobe-beef restaurants (especially dinner and weekends).

---

## 5. Himeji — Japan's most beautiful castle, fully intact
**Closest base:** Osaka *or* Kyoto · **Travel:** ~30 min (Osaka) / ~45 min (Kyoto) by shinkansen

**Getting there:** Sanyo Shinkansen Shin-Osaka → **Himeji** (~30 min); Kyoto → Himeji (~45 min). Budget option: JR Special Rapid from Osaka (~60 min, much cheaper). Castle is a straight 15–20 min walk down Otemae-dori from the station.

**Vibe:** A short, high-payoff trip built around one of the finest surviving original castles on Earth — the brilliant-white "White Heron Castle," a UNESCO site that survived war and never burned. Compact; easily combined with Kobe.

**Top things to do**
- **Himeji Castle** — climb the original six-story wooden keep (60–90 min with queues). **Admission rose to ¥2,500 (₹1,450) for adults on 1 March 2026; under-18s now free.**
- **Koko-en Garden** — nine walled Edo-style gardens beside the castle (¥400 / ₹232). **Combo ticket ¥2,600 (₹1,508)** saves ¥300.
- **West-side photo spot** — the classic full-cluster castle shot.
- **Otemae-dori** — the approach avenue for lunch and the reveal.

**Best for:** history/architecture fans, photographers, a half-day paired with Kobe or Arima.
**Plan:** Castle + Koko-en ≈ 2.5–3h. Hours 9:00–17:00 (last entry 16:30).
**October:** Ideal — comfortable, and you dodge cherry-blossom-season crowds; light foliage in the garden.
**Booking:** None; go early on weekends to beat keep queues.

## 6. Arima Onsen — Japan's oldest hot-spring town, just behind Kobe's mountain
**Closest base:** Kobe/Osaka · **Travel:** ~30–40 min from Kobe; ~60–70 min from Osaka

**Getting there:** From Shin-Kobe, subway to Tanigami → Shintetsu line to **Arima Onsen** (~30–40 min). From Osaka, direct **Hankyu express bus** (~60 min, ¥1,400 / ₹812) is simplest. You can also ride the **Rokko-Arima Ropeway** over Mt Rokko (¥1,850 / ₹1,073 round trip) — a scenic way in that pairs the night view with the baths.

**Vibe:** Tiny, atmospheric onsen village of narrow stone lanes — the classic "soak + stroll in a yukata" experience, remarkably close to the city.

**Top things to do**
- **Kin-no-yu (Gold Spring)** — bathe in the famous iron-rich brown "gold" water (day-use public bath).
- **Gin-no-yu (Silver Spring)** — clear carbonated/radium "silver" water; a Gold+Silver combo ticket exists.
- **Stroll the old town** — steaming source springs, sweet shops, tansan-senbei (carbonated crackers), footbaths.
- **Rokko Garden Terrace / night view** — ride the ropeway up Mt Rokko for the day or night panorama, then descend to soak.

**Best for:** a relaxed onsen half-day, couples, anyone wanting a real hot-spring town without a long haul.
**October:** Excellent — cool enough to enjoy hot water; foliage on Mt Rokko begins late month.
**Booking:** Day-use baths need no booking; bring cash (some shops are cash-only).

## 7. Koyasan / Mount Koya — sleep in a temple, walk a lantern-lit graveyard at night
**Closest base:** Osaka · **Travel:** ~1.5–2h from Namba

**Getting there:** Nankai Koya Line from **Namba** → Gokurakubashi (~80–90 min limited express), then a 5-min cable car up, then bus into town. The **Koyasan World Heritage Ticket** bundles the round-trip + local bus.

**Vibe:** A living Buddhist monastic town at 800m altitude, founded 1,200 years ago by Kobo Daishi — cedar forest, incense, temple bells. The single most transporting overnight near Kansai. *Doable as a day trip, but it's designed to be slept in.*

**Top things to do**
- **Shukubo (temple lodging)** — stay overnight in a working temple. Rates ~¥12,000–40,000 (₹7,000–23,200) pp including two meals. **Ekoin** and **Fudoin** are popular. This is the headline experience.
- **Shojin ryori** — the monks' vegetarian dinner & breakfast: koyadofu (freeze-dried tofu), yuba (tofu skin), konnyaku, seasonal veg. Served ~17:30–18:00, so arrive by 17:00.
- **Okunoin cemetery night tour** — a guided walk (often led by a resident monk) through 200,000+ moss-covered graves and lanterns to Kobo Daishi's mausoleum. Free to enter anytime, but the guided night tour is the magic.
- **Morning prayers & goma fire ritual** — join the ~6am chanting/fire ceremony (esp. at Ekoin) before breakfast.
- **Danjo Garan & Konpon Daito** — the vast vermilion central pagoda and temple complex.
- **Kongobu-ji** — head temple with Japan's largest rock garden.

**Best for:** slow travel, spirituality/meditation, a once-in-a-trip atmosphere. Ideal for a mindful overnight.
**October:** Great — crisp mountain air; foliage peaks late Oct–early Nov (cold at night, pack a layer).
**Booking:** **Book the shukubo well in advance** — weeks ahead in general, and 1–2+ months for October/autumn weekends. Reserve via Koyasan Shukubo Association (koyasan-shukubo.net) or Booking.com/Agoda; most temples are cash-only. This is the one place on the list where advance booking is essential.

## 8. Lake Biwa / Otsu / Hikone — a national-treasure castle, Japan's biggest lake, and a mountaintop museum
**Closest base:** Kyoto · **Travel:** ~10 min (Otsu) / ~50 min (Hikone) / ~65 min (Miho Museum)

**Getting there:** JR from Kyoto → **Otsu** (~10 min) for the lake's south shore; → **Hikone** (~50 min) for the castle. **Miho Museum:** JR to Ishiyama (~15 min, ¥240) then Teisan bus #150 (~50 min, ¥1,000 / ₹580). *Pick one anchor — doing all three in a day is too much.*

**Vibe:** Shiga prefecture, next door to Kyoto but far quieter — big-sky lake scenery, a rare original castle, and a genuinely jaw-dropping I.M. Pei museum hidden in the hills. Great crowd escape.

**Top things to do**
- **Hikone Castle** — one of only five castles designated National Treasures, with its original keep intact. Ticket ¥1,000 / ₹580 includes **Genkyu-en**, a beautiful 1677 lord's garden.
- **Miho Museum** — I.M. Pei's mountaintop masterpiece reached through a cherry-lined tunnel and suspension bridge; world-class antiquities in a stunning building. ~2h; open 10:00–17:00 (last entry 16:00), seasonal opening dates so check ahead.
- **Lake Biwa activities** — cruise boats, lakeside cycling, SUP/kayak in season, and the **Biwako Valley** ropeway + zipline/skywalk for views. Otsu's Michigan paddle-steamer cruise is a classic.
- **Shirahige Shrine** — the "floating" torii gate standing in the lake (west shore; photogenic, a bit farther).
- **Omihachiman** — canal town with boat rides and old merchant streets (add-on if you have time).

**Best for:** repeat visitors, art & design lovers, cyclists, anyone wanting Kyoto-adjacent calm.
**October:** Lovely — clear lake days, comfortable cycling; the Miho approach and Hikone garden colour up late Oct–Nov.
**Booking:** None generally; **verify Miho Museum's seasonal open dates** before going (it closes between exhibitions).

## 9. Wakayama City / Kishu — castle, a "Laputa" island fortress, and a tuna-cutting market
**Closest base:** Osaka · **Travel:** ~1h from Namba

**Getting there:** Nankai **Limited Express "Southern"** from Namba → Wakayama-shi (~60 min). Castle is a ~10-min walk from the station.

**Vibe:** An under-touristed coastal prefecture capital — former seat of the Kishu Tokugawa clan. Real-Japan feel, sea air, and a cult-favourite ruined island fortress. A good pick if you want somewhere most tourists skip.

**Top things to do**
- **Wakayama Castle** — reconstructed Kishu-Tokugawa castle with sweeping views over the river, city and Kii Channel; nice grounds and a red "Ohashi Roka" corridor bridge.
- **Tomogashima Island** — 20-min ferry from Kada; hike among overgrown WWII-era brick forts and tunnels that inspired *Castle in the Sky (Laputa)* vibes. Bring shoes for scrambling.
- **Kuroshio Market (Wakayama Marina City)** — watch the theatrical **tuna-cutting show** (3x daily) and eat ultra-fresh seafood.
- **Porto Europa** — a free-entry mock-European harbour theme park at Marina City (pay per ride); fun, kitsch photo spot.
- **Wakayama ramen (Chuka soba)** — the city's famous soy-tonkotsu ramen; a must-eat.

**Best for:** off-the-beaten-path explorers, ruin/hiking fans (Tomogashima), seafood lovers.
**October:** Good — mild coast weather; Tomogashima ferries run more reliably outside winter, but always check the day's schedule/weather.
**Booking:** None, but confirm Tomogashima ferry times/weather in advance.

## 10. Minoo (Minoh) Falls — a 33m waterfall hike and deep-fried maple leaves, 30 min from Osaka
**Closest base:** Osaka · **Travel:** ~30 min from Umeda

**Getting there:** Hankyu Takarazuka Line from **Osaka-Umeda** → Ishibashi-handai-mae (~15 min) → transfer to Minoo Line → **Minoo Station** (~5 min).

**Vibe:** An astonishingly easy nature escape — a paved 2.7km forest trail along a river straight to a clean, single-drop waterfall. Wild monkeys, temples, and a famous snack en route. Perfect low-effort half-day in the trees.

**Top things to do**
- **Minoo Falls** — a 33m curtain of water into a forest plunge pool; the trail's payoff, ~40–50 min easy walk each way from the station.
- **Momiji tempura (fried maple leaves)** — the local specialty: batter-fried, salted maple leaves sold at stalls along the trail (a ~1,300-year-old tradition). Snack of the day.
- **Ryuanji Temple & Minoo Park** — riverside shrines and forest scenery en route.
- **Insect Museum / observation spots** — quirky stops; watch for wild monkeys (don't feed them).

**Best for:** an easy nature/hike half-day, foodie-curious walkers, a cheap breather from the city.
**October:** Green early-mid month; **the real show is mid-Nov–early Dec** when the maples blaze — one of the Osaka area's best foliage spots. Go weekday/early morning or after 3pm in season to dodge crowds.
**Booking:** None. Free trail.

# Honest 2h+ calls — great, but *not* quick

These exceed the ~2-hour brief on travel time. Included because they're outstanding — just plan the day (or an overnight) around the journey.

## 11. Amanohashidate — one of Japan's "Three Great Views" *(~2h+ each way)*
**Closest base:** Kyoto · **Travel:** ~2h+ by direct **JR Hashidate Limited Express** from Kyoto Station (no transfers).

**Vibe:** A 3.6km pine-covered sandbar arcing across Miyazu Bay — designated one of Japan's Three Scenic Views since the 1600s. Doable as a long day trip; nicer with an overnight on the Sea of Japan.

**Top things to do**
- **Amanohashidate View Land** — chairlift/monorail up (round trip ¥850 / ₹493) for the classic panorama; do the "matanozoki" (view it upside-down through your legs, so the sandbar looks like a bridge to heaven).
- **Kasamatsu Park** — the opposite-side viewpoint where the sandbar resembles a rising dragon.
- **Walk or cycle the sandbar** — ~45 min on foot, faster by rental bike, under the pines.
- **Chion-ji Temple** — at the south end, known for fan-shaped omikuji fortunes.

**Best for:** scenery collectors, a slower coastal escape.
**October:** Great weather; foliage late. Plan ~3–5h on-site + ~4–5h round-trip travel — leave early.
**Booking:** None; consider an overnight to justify the distance.

## 12. Kinosaki Onsen — the definitive onsen-town stroll in a yukata *(~2.5–2h40 each way)*
**Closest base:** Kyoto or Osaka · **Travel:** ~2.5h from Kyoto / ~2h40 from Osaka by **direct Limited Express** (Kinosaki / Kounotori — no transfers).

**Vibe:** The archetypal Japanese hot-spring town — a willow-lined canal you wander in a yukata and wooden geta, hopping between historic public bathhouses. Magical, but too far for a relaxed *pure* day trip; **best as a 1-night stay** (day trips work but feel rushed).

**Top things to do**
- **Soto-yu bath-hopping** — seven traditional public baths (currently six open; Sato-no-yu is being rebuilt). Each has its own character.
- **Yumepa day pass** — unlimited entry to all public baths ¥1,500 / ₹870 (adults).
- **Canal stroll in yukata** — the whole town is the attraction; footbaths, sweet shops, Tajima beef and snow-crab in season.
- **Kinosaki ropeway** — up Mt Daishi for town-and-sea views.

**Best for:** onsen lovers, couples, a restorative overnight.
**October:** Beautiful onsen weather **but October–December is peak season** — book lodging early and expect crowds. (Snow crab season also starts early Nov.)
**Booking:** **Reserve a ryokan well ahead** for October; day-trippers just buy the Yumepa on arrival.

## 13. Nachi Falls & Kumano Nachi Taisha — Japan's tallest waterfall + a UNESCO pilgrimage *(~4h+ each way — really an overnight)*
**Closest base:** Osaka (or Kyoto) · **Travel:** ~4h from Osaka / ~4.5h from Kyoto by JR Limited Express to Kii-Katsuura + bus.

**Vibe:** The postcard of spiritual Japan — a 133m waterfall (Japan's tallest single drop) with the vermilion Seiganto-ji pagoda in the foreground, on the sacred **Kumano Kodo** pilgrimage route. Genuinely bucket-list, genuinely far.

**Top things to do**
- **Nachi Falls (Nachi-no-Otaki)** — the 133m sacred cascade; view it with the three-storied pagoda for the iconic shot.
- **Kumano Nachi Taisha & Seiganto-ji** — grand shrine-temple complex above the falls, part of the Kumano Sanzan.
- **Daimon-zaka** — a short, gorgeous moss-and-cedar cobbled stretch of the ancient Kumano Kodo pilgrim path you can actually walk.
- **Katsuura tuna & onsen** — Kii-Katsuura is a top tuna port with seaside hot springs (great overnight base).

**Best for:** pilgrims, serious scenery/hiking fans, slow travellers.
**October:** Lush and good for walking; foliage later.
**Honest verdict:** A day trip means ~9–10h of travel and a strict last-bus deadline — **do it as an overnight**, or take an organized ~12h tour bus from Osaka/Kyoto if you must same-day.
**Booking:** Book ryokan/tour ahead; confirm last bus times obsessively if day-tripping.

| You want… | Go to |
|---|---|
| Iconic + easy first trip | **Nara** |
